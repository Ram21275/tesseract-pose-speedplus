\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ground-truth crops.} All results use ground-truth boxes. For translation in particular, the box alone gives $0.55$\,m, so a deployable claim needs a detector or perturbed boxes.
\item \textbf{Comparisons.} External SPEED+ numbers are not matched comparisons.
\item \textbf{Quantisation floor.} The depth-5 leaf quantisation ($2.51^\circ$ mean) bounds fine accuracy, and our best synthetic errors ($3.3$--$3.7^\circ$) are close to it. A continuous refinement on top of the selected mode is a natural next step; the refiners we tried helped only on synthetic data.
\item \textbf{Cost.} Orbit voting costs $4\times$ inference.
\item \textbf{Scope of the components.} MoGe-2 fusion does not help on every dataset. YCB-V trained on real videos alone does not generalise, and on T-LESS and SYMSOL-T we remain behind fine-tuned diffusion models.
\item \textbf{Timing.} Timings come from a shared GPU and are upper bounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
A small tesseract tree on frozen DINOv3 and MoGe-2 features, fused by a training-free product of experts, is an accurate rotation predictor on synthetic spacecraft images. Its failures on real images are mostly flips to a look-alike mode that the model already considers. Scoring hierarchical hypotheses for consistency across exactly mapped in-plane rotations repairs most of these flips, and distilling that consistency into single-view students improves the result further, to $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$ on the SPEED+ real domains. Pre-registered ablations attribute the gains to the tree (precision), to photometric augmentation and orbit voting (real-domain flips), and to distillation (both), and they rule out several plausible alternatives. Our evaluation is intentionally conservative. We hope the protocol and the negative results are as useful as the method.
