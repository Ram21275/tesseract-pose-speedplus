\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Ground-truth crops.} The reported results use ground-truth boxes; a synthetic-only detector for SPEED+ is being evaluated (\cref{tab:spp_sota}). For translation in particular, the box alone gives $0.55$\,m on SPEED+, so a deployable claim needs detected boxes.
\item \textbf{Translation beyond SPEED+.} Translation blocks for T-LESS, SYMSOL-T and YCB-V are still being evaluated, so these datasets are currently compared on rotation only.
\item \textbf{Matched comparisons.} Published numbers use different crops, masks, object sets and fine-tuned backbones; \cref{tab:tless_sota,tab:symsol_sota,tab:spp_sota} state each setting, and only rows with the same setting are matched.
\item \textbf{Hierarchy vs.\ grid.} The flat-baseline comparison of \cref{sec:abl_flat} uses a coarser grid than the tree; the same-grid control is still running, so the share of the gain due to hierarchical prediction is not yet established.
\item \textbf{Quantisation floor.} The depth-5 leaf quantisation ($2.51^\circ$ mean) bounds fine accuracy for asymmetric objects. Our best synthetic SPEED+ errors ($3.3$--$3.7^\circ$) are close to it, and it caps fine-threshold recall on T-LESS. A continuous refinement on top of the selected mode is a natural next step; the refiners we tried helped only on synthetic data (\cref{sec:supp_negative}).
\item \textbf{Per-object heads.} One tree is trained per object; there is no zero-shot transfer to new objects (\cref{sec:supp_zeroshot}).
\item \textbf{Cost.} Orbit voting costs $4\times$ inference, and MoGe-2 fusion does not help on every dataset.
\item \textbf{Timing.} Timings come from a shared GPU and are upper bounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
A small tesseract tree on frozen DINOv3 and MoGe-2 features is a competitive basis for 6D object pose when its hypotheses are \emph{selected} well. Three components target selection: multi-path targets that spread the training signal over all symmetric equivalents, orbit voting that scores hierarchical hypotheses for consistency across exactly mapped in-plane rotations, and orbit distillation that moves this consistency into single-view students. Multi-path targets improve T-LESS at every recall threshold and every SYMSOL-T shape and, unlike canonical labels, remain compatible with orbit voting. On the nearly symmetric SPEED+ target, orbit voting and distillation repair most real-domain flips. Synthetic training data turns YCB-V from a failure into a working model. Fine-tuned methods remain more accurate on all four benchmarks, and our evaluation is intentionally conservative; we hope the protocol, the matched comparisons and the negative results are as useful as the method.
