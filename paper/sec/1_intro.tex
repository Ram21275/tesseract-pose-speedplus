\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating the 6D pose of a known object from one image underlies robotic grasping, augmented reality and autonomous rendezvous. A large part of the remaining error comes from \emph{ambiguity}. A symmetric object has several rotations that produce the same image, and every one of them is correct. A nearly symmetric object has look-alike rotations that differ only in small details, and only one of them is correct. Under a domain shift (renders to real images, new lighting), those small details are exactly what becomes unreliable. Distributional pose methods~\cite{murphy2021implicitpdf,haugaard2023spyropose,hsiao2024liepose} can represent such multi-modal posteriors, but a single prediction still has to \emph{select} a mode.

We study this selection problem with a deliberately lightweight design built on \emph{frozen} foundation models. DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} gives robust appearance features, and MoGe-2~\cite{wang2025moge2} gives per-pixel surface normals. A small head predicts the rotation as a path in a hierarchical partition of $SO(3)$ derived from the recursive tesseract subdivision~\cite{kurz2017tesseract}. It is an autoregressive Transformer that first chooses one of 4 charts, then one of 8 children at each of 5 levels ($131{,}072$ cells), attending to the frozen tokens. The DINOv3 and MoGe-2 trees are fused per level by a training-free product of experts (PoE)~\cite{hinton2002poe}, and a translation block on the same tokens completes the 6D pose. Only 2.4\,M parameters per branch are trained.

Hierarchical hypotheses make selection explicit, and we attack it at three points. \emph{During training}, the target of a symmetric object is the whole set of equivalent rotations, so the tree learns to split its probability between them instead of fitting an arbitrary label (\cref{sec:symtarget}). \emph{At test time}, we use the fact that rotating the crop in the image plane is an exact camera roll. A wrong look-alike pose tends to win only in some rotated views, whereas the true pose, mapped back through the known transform, is consistent across all of them. \emph{Orbit voting} collects the tree's beam candidates from four rotated views and scores each candidate by its tree likelihood as seen from every view (\cref{sec:orbit}). Finally, \emph{orbit distillation} trains single-view students towards the orbit system's consensus (\cref{sec:distill}).

An audit on the synthetic-to-real benchmark SPEED+~\cite{park2022speedplus} motivates the design (\cref{sec:diagnosis}). Its target spacecraft is nearly symmetric. The real-domain error of the baseline is dominated by $180^\circ$ flips about a few body axes, and an oracle that picks the best of the tree's own candidates reaches $9.8^\circ$, against $26.9^\circ$ for the prediction. The correct mode is usually present; it is not selected.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A lightweight hierarchical 6D pose predictor on frozen DINOv3 and MoGe-2 features, with training-free per-level PoE fusion. In a matched comparison it is far more accurate than a flat 131k-anchor classifier, with $15\times$ fewer parameters (\cref{sec:abl_flat}).
\item Symmetry-aware multi-path targets for hierarchical rotation trees. They reduce the SYMSOL-T error by 20--30\% relative to single or canonical labels, and, unlike canonical labels, remain compatible with orbit voting (\cref{sec:abl_sym}).
\item Orbit voting with an exactly derived roll transform for hierarchical candidates, and orbit distillation into single-view students. On SPEED+ they repair 62--80\% of severe flips while breaking 0.4\% of good predictions, and reduce the real-domain error to $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$ (\cref{sec:spp}).
\item An evaluation on four benchmarks (T-LESS, SYMSOL-T, YCB-V, SPEED+) with comparisons matched to published protocols where possible, three seeds, pre-registered criteria, selection on validation data only, and full reporting of negative results (\cref{sec:experiments,sec:ablations}).
\end{itemize}
Symmetry-aware losses, test-time augmentation and self-distillation are established ideas. Our claim concerns their exact geometric form for hierarchical rotation hypotheses and their measured effect on mode selection, not the ideas themselves. We do not claim a new state of the art: fine-tuned methods remain more accurate on every benchmark we use.
