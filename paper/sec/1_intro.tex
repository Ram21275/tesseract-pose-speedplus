\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating the 6D pose of a known object from one image underlies robotic grasping and bin picking, augmented reality, inspection and autonomous docking. Each of these applications has its own objects, sensors and lighting, and today each typically fine-tunes its own network. Foundation backbones change this trade-off: their features already encode appearance and geometry well enough that, in principle, one frozen backbone and one core pose algorithm could serve every application, with only a small \emph{downstream head} trained per object or product. We call this setting \emph{artificial specific intelligence}: a shared general-purpose model specialised to many narrow tasks by small, cheap, separately deployable heads. This paper asks how far such a design gets on 6D object pose, and what has to be solved to make it work.

A large part of the remaining error comes from \emph{ambiguity}. A symmetric object has several rotations that produce the same image, and every one of them is correct. A nearly symmetric object has look-alike rotations that differ only in small details, and only one of them is correct. Under a domain shift (renders to real images, new lighting), those small details are exactly what becomes unreliable. Distributional pose methods~\cite{murphy2021implicitpdf,haugaard2023spyropose,hsiao2024liepose} can represent such multi-modal posteriors, but a single prediction still has to \emph{select} a mode.

Our design keeps both backbones frozen. DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} gives robust appearance features, and MoGe-2~\cite{wang2025moge2} gives per-pixel surface normals. The downstream head predicts the rotation as a path in a hierarchical partition of $SO(3)$ derived from the recursive tesseract subdivision~\cite{kurz2017tesseract}. It is an autoregressive Transformer that first chooses one of 4 charts, then one of 8 children at each of 5 levels ($131{,}072$ cells), attending to the frozen tokens. The DINOv3 and MoGe-2 trees are fused per level by a training-free product of experts (PoE)~\cite{hinton2002poe}, and a translation block on the same tokens completes the 6D pose. Only 2.4\,M parameters per branch are trained, about 9\,MB at fp32; the backbone, the training recipe and the inference algorithm are the same for every application.

Hierarchical hypotheses make selection explicit, and we attack it at three points. \emph{During training}, the target of a symmetric object is the whole set of equivalent rotations, so the tree learns to split its probability between them instead of fitting an arbitrary label (\cref{sec:symtarget}). \emph{At test time}, we use the fact that rotating the crop in the image plane is an exact camera roll. A wrong look-alike pose tends to win only in some rotated views, whereas the true pose, mapped back through the known transform, is consistent across all of them. \emph{Orbit voting} collects the tree's beam candidates from four rotated views and scores each candidate by its tree likelihood as seen from every view (\cref{sec:orbit}). Finally, \emph{orbit distillation} trains single-view students towards the orbit system's consensus (\cref{sec:distill}).

We evaluate on three general-object benchmarks that stress different kinds of ambiguity: textureless industrial parts with continuous and discrete symmetries (T-LESS), exactly symmetric solids (SYMSOL-T) and cluttered household objects (YCB-V). We then apply the same design to a demanding downstream application: spacecraft pose under a synthetic-to-real shift (SPEED+~\cite{park2022speedplus}), where only renders are available for training. There, an audit shows that the real-domain errors are dominated by $180^\circ$ flips of a nearly symmetric target, and that an oracle picking the best of the tree's own candidates reaches $9.8^\circ$, against $26.9^\circ$ for the prediction (\cref{sec:diagnosis}): the correct mode is usually present but not selected.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A frozen-backbone 6D pose design in which only a small hierarchical head (2.4\,M parameters per branch) is trained per application, with training-free per-level PoE fusion of appearance and geometry. The same backbone, recipe and inference algorithm are used on all four benchmarks; per-application options (fusion, orbit voting) are chosen on validation data. We report the head's deployment cost and its accuracy under reduced precision (\cref{app:deploy}).
\item Symmetry-aware multi-path targets for hierarchical rotation trees. They improve T-LESS at every recall threshold and every SYMSOL-T shape, and, unlike canonical labels, remain compatible with orbit voting (\cref{sec:general,sec:abl_sym}).
\item Orbit voting with an exactly derived roll transform for hierarchical candidates, and orbit distillation into single-view students. On the spacecraft application they repair 62--80\% of severe flips while breaking 0.4\% of good predictions, and reduce the real-domain error to $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$ (\cref{sec:spp}).
\item An evaluation with comparisons matched to published protocols where possible, three seeds, pre-registered criteria, selection on validation data only, and full reporting of negative results, including where the shared design does \emph{not} transfer: orbit voting hurts on YCB-V, MoGe-2 fusion does not help everywhere, and heads do not transfer zero-shot to new objects (\cref{sec:experiments,sec:ablations}).
\end{itemize}
Symmetry-aware losses, test-time augmentation and self-distillation are established ideas. Our claim concerns their exact geometric form for hierarchical rotation hypotheses and their measured effect on mode selection, not the ideas themselves. We do not claim a new state of the art: fine-tuned methods remain more accurate on every benchmark we use.
