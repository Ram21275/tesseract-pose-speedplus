\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Estimating the pose of an uncooperative spacecraft from a single image is a prerequisite for autonomous rendezvous, servicing and debris removal. Because real orbital imagery with ground truth is scarce, models are trained on synthetic renders and must transfer to real images with harsh, directional illumination. SPEED+~\cite{park2022speedplus} makes this gap measurable: models train on synthetic images and are tested on hardware-in-the-loop \emph{lightbox} and \emph{sunlamp} captures of a physical mock-up. Even strong pipelines lose much of their accuracy there~\cite{park2024spnv2,dutta2026spacehop}.

We approach the problem from the side of \emph{frozen} foundation models. DINOv3~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} gives robust appearance features, and MoGe-2~\cite{wang2025moge2} gives per-pixel surface normals. If such features already carry the pose information, a small head trained on synthetic data could transfer better than a fine-tuned network that can over-fit to render statistics. Our head predicts a rotation as a path in a hierarchical partition of $SO(3)$ derived from the recursive tesseract subdivision~\cite{kurz2017tesseract}. It is an autoregressive Transformer that first chooses one of 4 charts, then one of 8 children at each of 5 levels ($131{,}072$ cells), attending to the frozen tokens. The DINOv3 and MoGe-2 trees are fused per level by a training-free product of experts (PoE)~\cite{hinton2002poe}.

This baseline is accurate on synthetic data ($5.5^\circ$) but not on real images ($26.9^\circ$ lightbox, $36.7^\circ$ sunlamp). A careful audit (\cref{sec:diagnosis}) shows that the real-domain error is not spread over all rotations. It is concentrated in $180^\circ$ flips about a few body axes of the nearly symmetric spacecraft, at about twice the rate expected for random errors. Crucially, the correct mode is usually present among the predictor's own hypotheses: an oracle that picks the best of the tree's candidates and their flips reaches $9.8^\circ$ on lightbox. The failure is one of \emph{selection}. Continuous $SO(3)$ flow posteriors, flow-based re-scoring, template matching and discrete path flows did not fix it (\cref{sec:supp_negative}).

What does fix most of it is a simple, geometry-exact form of test-time consistency that we call \emph{orbit voting} (\cref{sec:orbit}). Rotating the crop in the image plane is equivalent to rolling the camera about the ray through the crop centre, so the true rotation transforms by a known left multiplication. A wrong look-alike pose tends to win only in some of the rotated views, whereas the true pose, mapped back, is consistent across all of them. We therefore collect the tree's beam candidates from four rotated views, map them to a common frame, and score each candidate by its tree likelihood as seen from every view. Training with photometric augmentation and in-plane rotations, labelled through the same transform, and then applying orbit voting reduces the error to $12.3^\circ$ on both real domains.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
\item A lightweight hierarchical rotation predictor on frozen DINOv3 and MoGe-2 features, with training-free per-level PoE fusion. In a matched comparison it is far more accurate than a flat 131k-anchor Hopf classifier of the kind used in recent spacecraft work~\cite{dutta2026spacehop}, with $15\times$ fewer parameters (\cref{sec:abl_flat}).
\item A diagnosis that real-domain error on SPEED+ is dominated by flip \emph{mode selection}, with the correct mode usually among the hypotheses (\cref{sec:diagnosis}).
\item Orbit voting with an exactly derived roll transform for hierarchical candidates. With photometric augmentation it repairs 62--80\% of severe flips while breaking 0.4\% of good predictions. Orbit distillation improves the single-view students ($17.3^\circ/20.2^\circ\to15.9^\circ/18.3^\circ$) and, combined with orbit voting, reaches $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$ (\cref{sec:orbit,sec:distill}).
\item A pre-registered evaluation protocol with three seeds, selection on synthetic validation only, and full reporting of negative results. It includes the evidence that photometric augmentation, not rotation augmentation, carries the real-domain gain (\cref{sec:abl_aug}). It also covers a 6-DoF extension (\cref{sec:sixdof}) and transfer to T-LESS, SYMSOL-T and YCB-V (\cref{sec:xds}).
\end{itemize}
Augmentation, test-time augmentation and self-distillation are established techniques. Our claim is about their exact geometric form for hierarchical rotation hypotheses and their measured effect on a diagnosed failure mode, not about the techniques themselves.
