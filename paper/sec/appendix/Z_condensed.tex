\subsection{Method details}\label{app:z_method}

\paragraph{Tesseract partition.}
A unit quaternion $q$ is assigned to the chart $c=\arg\max_i|q_i|$ (first maximum on ties) and multiplied by $\operatorname{sign}(q_c)$, so $q$ and $-q$ get the same chart and the same path. The quaternion is projected to the cube facet, $x=q/|q_c|$, and its three free coordinates $u\in[-1,1]^3$ are halved recursively. At each level, each coordinate goes to the upper half ($b_i=1$) if $u_i$ is at least the interval midpoint, which gives the child id $k=b_0+2b_1+4b_2\in\{0,\dots,7\}$. The leaf id at depth $L$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{id}=c\cdot 8^L+\textstyle\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}k_{\ell}\,8^{L-\ell},
\end{equation}
so there are $4\cdot 8^L$ leaves. After $l$ decisions, free coordinate $i$ of the cell centre is $u_i=-1+\sum_{t=1}^{l}b_{t,i}2^{1-t}+2^{-l}$; the centre quaternion sets $x_c=1$ and normalises. The tree uses cell centres only. This follows the recursive tesseract of Kurz \etal~\cite{kurz2017tesseract}, but uses cube centres instead of corners (a corner has no unique parent), keeps only the 4 positive facets, and adds an explicit encoder, decoder and tie rule. The encoder invariants (antipodal invariance, encode--decode round trip, consistent prefixes) hold on all 69,491 SPEED+ labels.
% src: src/tfpose/tesseract.py (canonicalize, project, encode, leaf_id, _cell_bounds, _lift, decode); outputs/reports/EXP-001_gt_tesseract_paths.md

\Cref{app:z_tab_depth} gives the quantisation by depth. The error halves with every level. The grid is not uniform: at L5 cell volumes differ by up to $14.4\times$, so a uniform prior over leaves is not uniform over rotations. We stop at $L=5$: L6 would have 1,048,576 cells against 47,966 SPEED+ training images. Per hypothesis, the Tesseract is less precise than Super-Fibonacci and Hopf grids~\cite{yershova2010hopf} of the same size (L5: 2.51$^\circ$ mean against 2.47$^\circ$ for Super-Fibonacci). Its advantage is search cost: greedy descent scores only 44 nodes at L5.

\paragraph{Frozen features.}
Both backbones are frozen (evaluation mode, no gradients, asserted after every pass). Features are extracted once per crop and view into an fp16 cache, and the trees read only these caches.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{DINOv3, \texttt{tokens8}.} DINOv3 ViT-L/16~\cite{simeoni2025dinov3} on a $256\times256$ crop with ImageNet normalisation. The last-block patch tokens ($16\times16\times1024$; CLS and register tokens dropped) are average-pooled to $8\times8$, giving $64\times1024$ tokens.
  \item \textbf{MoGe-2, \texttt{normals16\textasciitilde and}.} MoGe-2~\cite{wang2025moge2} on a $518\times518$ crop predicts camera-frame normals $n$ and a mask, both pooled to $74\times74$. The \emph{consensus mask} $m$ is the AND of the MoGe-2 mask ($>0.5$) and a training-free DINOv3 foreground: the first principal component of the 256 patch tokens, sign-flipped so the border is background, upsampled and Otsu-thresholded. The tokens are $X=[\mathrm{pool}_{16}(n\odot m),\,\mathrm{pool}_{16}(m)]\in\mathbb{R}^{256\times4}$. On real images the AND mask cuts the foreground outside the keypoint envelope from 0.52 to 0.12 (LB) and from 0.57 to 0.12 (SL) compared with MoGe-2's own mask.
  \item \textbf{Standardisation.} Every feature dimension is standardised as $(x-\mu)/(\mathrm{sd}+10^{-6})$ with statistics from the clean training rows only (per object on the non-SPEED+ datasets), and the same statistics are applied to augmented, validation and test views. The MoGe-2 trees for three SYMSOL-T shapes use an sd floor of $10^{-2}$ (\cref{app:z_history}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Tree network.}
Each token is mapped by LayerNorm and a linear layer to $d=256$, and a fixed 2-D sinusoidal encoding is added. Its frequencies are $\omega_k=2^k\pi$ for $k=0,\dots,63$, \ie up to $2^{63}\pi$; only the lowest few ($2^k<2g$ for a $g\times g$ grid) separate grid positions, and in float32 the higher channels hold fixed, position-dependent rounding residues. One pre-norm Transformer encoder layer produces the memory, and a causal decoder with 2 layers (4 heads, FFN 512, dropout 0.1) reads the sequence $(r,\,s_1,\dots,s_L)$: a learned root token, then $s_{j+1}=E_{\text{lev}}[j]+W\varphi(c_j)$, where $c_j$ is the centre of the cell reached after $j$ decisions and $\varphi$ is the quaternion plus a 6-frequency Fourier encoding (52 dimensions). Output 0 feeds a 4-way chart head and output $j\ge1$ an 8-way child head. Under teacher forcing one decoder pass gives all $1+L$ distributions. The DINOv3 tree has 2,391,564 trainable parameters and the MoGe-2 tree 2,128,404.
% src: src/tfpose/phase3.py (_pos2d, HierTransformer); src/tfpose/predictor.py (fourier); outputs/experiments/EXP-036_transformer_soft_targets/RUN-*/status.json and EXP-109_*/RUN-*/status.json (n_params)

\paragraph{Soft targets, beam search and PoE.}
With $\delta(q,c)=2\arccos|\langle q,c\rangle|$ in degrees, the target at level $\ell$ is
\begin{equation}
  t^{(\ell)}_k=\mathrm{softmax}_k\big(-(\delta(q,c_{\ell,k})/\tau_\ell)^2\big),
\end{equation}
over the 4 chart centres ($\ell=0$) or the 8 children of the \emph{ground-truth} parent, so mass never leaves the true parent cell. The widths are fixed a priori to the mean cell error of each level: $\tau_0,\dots,\tau_5=39.27, 39.27, 19.95, 10.01, 5.01, 2.51^\circ$. The loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{GT}}$ sums the six soft cross-entropies. Beam search keeps the $B$ best prefixes by cumulative log-probability; greedy is $B=1$, and beams up to 8 did not change the single-view result (PoE synth 6.242$^\circ$ at $B{=}1$, 6.248$^\circ$ at $B{=}8$). The two branches are fused at every node by the parameter-free product of experts~\cite{hinton2002poe},
\begin{equation}
  \log\tilde p^{(\ell)}=\log\mathrm{softmax}\Big(\textstyle\sum_{b}\log\mathrm{softmax}(z_b^{(\ell)})\Big).
\end{equation}
% src: src/tfpose/phase3.py (TAU_DEG, soft_targets, soft_ce); src/tfpose/fusion.py (combine, fused_beam); outputs/reports/EXP-034_beam_decoding.md

\paragraph{Augmentation and the exact roll.}
Every training crop has two pre-extracted augmented copies (seeds 1 and 2). Each copy draws a roll $\theta\sim\mathcal{U}(-180^\circ,180^\circ)$ and a photometric chain: brightness, contrast, gamma, blur ($p=0.5$), glare blobs ($p=0.3$), Gaussian noise, random erasing ($p=0.3$) and JPEG ($p=0.5$). The rolled crop is resampled from the full image in one warp, so there are no black corners. A roll $\theta$ (counter-clockwise on screen) about the unit ray $d$ through the crop centre changes the labels as
\begin{equation}
  q'=\Exp(-\theta d)\otimes q,\qquad t'=\mathrm{Rot}(d,-\theta)\,t
\end{equation}
(the ``pre$-$'' convention). It was derived from the camera geometry and checked before any training on 512 synth crops at 4 angles: keypoints agree to 0.34\,px mean (1.05\,px p95), the label error is $0.06^\circ$ mean ($1.14^\circ$ max), and the other three conventions are off by 94--242\,px. On SPEED+, $d$ is computed from the \emph{undistorted} crop centre. On T-LESS and YCB-V objects lie far from the optical axis and the affine warp failed the same rule (p95 label error $1.43^\circ$ / $2.40^\circ$), so the crop is warped by the exact roll homography $C\,K\,\mathrm{Rot}(d,-\theta)\,K^{-1}$. The labels then follow exactly (error $0.000^\circ$), and the allocentric target is recomputed from $t'$. SYMSOL-T uses the full frame, whose centre is the principal point, so the affine roll is already exact. A roll multiplies on the left, so it commutes with right-multiplication by an object symmetry.
% src: src/tfpose/inplane.py (photometric, crop_rotated, crop_rolled_homography, rotate_label, ray_undistorted); scripts/exp109_convention_geometry.py; outputs/experiments/exp109_convention.txt; outputs/reports/EXP-109_real_domain_aug_tokens16_orbit.md (amendment 2026-10-15); outputs/reports/EXP-064_broader_object_validation.md (amendment 2026-10-18); src/tfpose/xobj.py

\paragraph{Orbit voting and scoring rules.}
At test time four views $\theta_k\in\{0,90,180,270\}^\circ$ are extracted. In each, the fused tree runs beam search with $B=8$, and the 8 leaf centres are mapped back to view 0 by the inverse roll (on allocentric datasets via the egocentric frame, using the ground-truth translation). This gives 32 candidates. The \emph{orbit product} maps each candidate $c$ into every view, encodes it to its own depth-5 path, and sums the teacher-forced fused log-probabilities:
\begin{equation}
  s(c)=\textstyle\sum_{k}\big[\log\tilde p_k^{(0)}(\text{chart})+\sum_{\ell=1}^{5}\log\tilde p_k^{(\ell)}(k_\ell\mid\text{prefix})\big].
\end{equation}
The prediction is $\arg\max_c s(c)$. EXP-114 compared it with three simpler rules over the same candidates: \emph{max-confidence} (highest beam score in its own view), \emph{top-1 vote} (Gaussian-kernel support, $\sigma=5^\circ$, among the four view top-1s) and \emph{weighted vote} (kernel support from all 32 candidates, weighted by the per-view softmax of beam scores). The product re-scoring costs 8.2\,ms (DINOv3-only) or 15.3\,ms (PoE) per image at batch 1; the weighted vote needs below 0.1\,ms.
% src: scripts/orbit_eval.py (tree_lp, evaluate: orbit_product, orbit_maxconf, orbit_vote1, orbit_vote32); scripts/exp064_eval.py; outputs/reports/EXP-114_orbit_scoring_rules.md

\paragraph{Symmetry-aware targets.}
For an object with symmetry set $\mathcal{S}$, three label modes were compared (EXP-106):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{A0, single label}: the annotated rotation $q_v$ of the view.
  \item \textbf{A1, canonical}: in each view separately, the equivalent $q_v\otimes s$ closest to the identity. Because it is per view, the labels of a view and its rolled copy need not be related by the roll transform.
  \item \textbf{A2, multi-path}: every equivalent $q_v\otimes s$ gets its own path and soft targets, and the loss is the mean of $\mathcal{L}_{\text{GT}}$ over them (all of $\mathcal{S}$ if $|\mathcal{S}|\le8$, otherwise 8 drawn per sample and step). For $|\mathcal{S}|=1$ this is exactly A0.
\end{itemize}
The pre-registration named a hard-path level-marginal loss for A2. An amendment written before any run replaced it by the smoothed loss above, because the hard-path loss would not reduce to A0 for asymmetric objects.
% src: scripts/train_xobj.py (--label, --sym-k, canon, multi_loss); outputs/reports/EXP-106_tless_multipath_tree.md (amendment 2026-10-27)

\paragraph{Orbit distillation.}
The teacher is the frozen orbit system of the same seed and variant, applied to each training image over its three existing views (clean and two augmented copies, with known rolls). It yields 24 candidates $c_j$ with weights $w_j=\mathrm{softmax}_j\,s(c_j)$, computed once and stored as constants. A new student tree is trained from scratch with
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\text{GT}}-\beta\textstyle\sum_{j}w_j\frac{1}{L+1}\sum_{\ell=0}^{L}\log p^{(\ell)}\big(\mathrm{pre}_\ell(c_j)\big),
\end{equation}
where $\log p^{(\ell)}(\mathrm{pre}_\ell(\cdot))$ is the joint log-probability of the length-$\ell$ prefix of the candidate's path, transported into the drawn view, and $\beta=1$ was fixed before any run. Inference is unchanged. This is data distillation~\cite{radosavovic2018data,hinton2015distilling} with a hierarchical target.
% src: scripts/exp117_teacher.py; scripts/train_consistency.py (mode distill, level_ce); outputs/reports/EXP-117_orbit_distillation.md (amendment 2026-10-17)

\paragraph{Translation block.}
We keep the SPACE-HOP target~\cite{dutta2026spacehop} unchanged. With the ground-truth square box $(b_x,b_y,b_w,b_h)$, image size $(W,H)$, $\alpha=1.6$ and $\kappa=\tfrac12(\alpha b_w/W+b_h/H)$, the target is $u=\big((f_xX/Z+c_x-b_x)/b_w,\;(f_yY/Z+c_y-b_y)/b_h,\;\kappa Z\big)$. A learned query cross-attends once (4 heads) to the tree's encoded tokens, followed by $\mathrm{LN}\to\mathrm{Linear}(256,256)\to\mathrm{GELU}\to\mathrm{Linear}(256,3)$: 331,011 parameters per branch. It is trained jointly with the tree ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{GT}}+\|u-u^*\|^2$; arm J) or on a frozen tree (arm F; also used on the other datasets, where $u$ is standardised). PoE averages the two branches' $u$, and orbit inference averages the four back-rolled estimates. The \emph{box-only} reference uses no image: object centre at the box centre, depth at the training mean.
% src: src/tfpose/trans.py (to_u, from_u, TransHead); scripts/train_probe_p3.py (--trans); scripts/exp118_eval.py; outputs/reports/EXP-118_translation_6dof.md

\paragraph{Flat baselines.}
The flat classifier is the same network with path decoding removed: the decoder runs on the root token alone and feeds one linear layer over $K$ anchors. For $K=131{,}076$ the head alone has 33,686,532 parameters (36,078,096 in total for DINOv3, against 2.4\,M for the tree). It is trained with geodesic soft targets over the 32 anchors nearest to the label at the tree's leaf width $2.51^\circ$, with the tree's optimiser, views and checkpoint rule. In orbit voting it scores a candidate by the log-probability of its hard anchor. Three grids are used: SPACE-HOP's Hopf grid at the count-matched $10{,}923\times12=131{,}076$ (EXP-113), the Tesseract's own 131,072 leaf centres, and a balanced Hopf grid $1{,}771\times74=131{,}054$ (EXP-127).

\paragraph{Metrics.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Rotation error} $E_R=2\arccos|\langle\hat q,q\rangle|$. The \emph{symmetry-aware} error (T-LESS, SYMSOL-T) is $\arccos\big(\tfrac12(\max_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\operatorname{tr}(\hat R^\top RS)-1)\big)$; on T-LESS the raw error is $38$--$47^\circ$, which is why it is needed.
  \item \textbf{acc@$k$ / R@$k$}: fraction with error below $k^\circ$ (strict on SPEED+, $\le$ elsewhere).
  \item \textbf{Severe flips}: fraction with $E_R>150^\circ$. Flip transitions against a reference: \emph{fixed} = share of its $>150^\circ$ errors that become $<20^\circ$, \emph{broken} = share of its $<20^\circ$ predictions that become $>150^\circ$.
  \item \textbf{Translation}: $E_T=\|\hat t-t\|$, normalised $e_t=E_T/\|t\|$, and T@$c$ = fraction with $E_T\le c$\,cm.
  \item \textbf{Official SPEED+ score}~\cite{park2022speedplus,park2024spnv2}: the mean over images of $e_q$ (rad) $+\,e_t$, with each term set to zero below the challenge floors ($0.169^\circ$, $2.173\times10^{-3}$).
  \item \textbf{Retention} of distillation: (base greedy $-$ student greedy) / (base greedy $-$ base orbit).
\end{itemize}
Outside SPEED+, errors are first averaged per object and then over objects with equal weight.

\paragraph{Selection and pre-registration.}
The split policy (DEC-000, restored by DEC-006) is: train on synthetic/train, select on synthetic validation (and from EXP-109 on also on val-aug), and treat lightbox and sunlamp as test-only and descriptive. A seeded 20\% lightbox split (DEC-005) was withdrawn the same day, before it influenced any run. The other benchmarks select on held-out scenes or sequences. Each experiment has a report that records, before any run, the question, arms, selection data, metrics and pass criterion. The standard pass rule compares 3-seed means on validation data: a difference passes only if it is larger than the larger of the two seed standard deviations. Protocol changes are dated amendments written before the affected runs; deviations found later are recorded as such. Real-domain and test numbers are reported for every arm and never used for selection.

\paragraph{Detected crops (EXP-124).}
The detected-crop evaluation was pre-registered before the detector was trained. An Ultralytics YOLO11s detector (COCO-pretrained) is trained on synthetic/train only, at image size 960 for 20 epochs, with the tight keypoint box as its single-class label and the checkpoint chosen on synthetic validation. At test time the top-1 box is turned into a crop with the ground-truth rule; when there is no detection the whole image is used, and these cases are counted. Systems S1--S3 were fixed before any result, and a ground-truth-crop run with the same code must first reproduce EXP-117 rotation and EXP-118 translation.

\paragraph{Compute.}
All logged runs used one NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada in a workstation shared with other users, so timings are upper bounds. Feature extraction on SPEED+ took 11.5\,ms per image for DINOv3 and 29.2\,ms for MoGe-2. \subsection{Deployment of the downstream head}\label{app:deploy}
Every application shares the same frozen backbones and the same inference algorithm; what is trained and shipped per application is only the downstream head: one tree per branch (DINOv3 and, for the PoE variant, MoGe-2) and, for 6D, the translation block. The head architecture is identical for every object and dataset, so its storage, latency and memory are properties of the design and are measured once. Its accuracy under reduced precision depends on the trained weights and is measured per application with each application's own evaluator and metric. This experiment (EXP-128) was pre-registered before any run; the frozen backbone is not quantised and its cost is reported separately (\cref{sec:supp_timing}).

\paragraph{Precisions.} fp32 is the reference. bf16 (autocast) is the precision of every other result in this paper. fp16 uses autocast. int8 is weight-only: every matmul weight of the head (all linear layers including the attention projections, 20 tensors per tree, $99.3\%$ of its parameters) is rounded to symmetric per-output-channel int8; activations, LayerNorms, embeddings and biases stay in fp32. Its accuracy is measured by simulating this rounding; its CPU speed is measured with PyTorch's dynamic int8 kernels, which convert the linear layers but not the attention in-projections. At fp32 the DINOv3 tree occupies about 9.1\,MB ($2{,}391{,}564$ parameters); with int8 weights the ideal size is about a quarter of that.

\paragraph{Criterion.} A precision's loss is \emph{negligible} if its mean error differs from fp32 by no more than the larger seed standard deviation, per application, inference mode and split (the pass rule used throughout the paper).

\subsection{Project history and decisions}\label{app:z_history}

\paragraph{Phase 0: geometry (EXP-000--003).}
EXP-000 fixed the SPEED+ pose convention $p_\text{cam}=R(q)\,p+r$ (keypoint boxes match with IoU 0.97 for $R$ against 0.52 for $R^\top$). EXP-001 verified the Tesseract encoder on every label, and EXP-002/003 measured its precision and compared it with other grids. The gate set $L_\text{max}=5$ and kept a Hopf grid as the mandatory matched baseline.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-000_loader_conventions.md; EXP-001_gt_tesseract_paths.md; EXP-002_depth_precision.md; EXP-003_grid_comparison.md

\paragraph{Phases 1--2: branches and fusion (EXP-010--029).}
On a 9,500-image subset, a hierarchical MLP probe on DINOv3 \texttt{grid4} reached a synth mean of 27.7$^\circ$, with real medians about $4\times$ worse. VGGT, the plan's geometric branch, was weaker everywhere and about $17\times$ slower, and MoGe-2 replaced it. A training-free consensus mask fixed MoGe-2's normals on real images. An equal-weight PoE of the two branches beat the best single branch (synth 25.9$^\circ$ vs 27.7$^\circ$), and no reliability weighting or learned fusion beat it.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-010..016, EXP-025, EXP-026, EXP-029 reports; outputs/DECISIONS.md (DEC-001, DEC-003, DEC-008)

\paragraph{Phase 3: the tree at full data (EXP-030--036).}
Training on all 47,966 images cut the error about $4\times$ (MLP tree, PoE 6.24$^\circ$). Of five decoder and target variants, the cross-attentive Transformer with geodesic soft targets (EXP-036) was best (PoE 5.540$^\circ$; LB 26.9$^\circ$, SL 36.6$^\circ$) and was frozen. DINOv3 alone was then better on synth (5.35$^\circ$) but far worse on real images. This exposed the selection-policy conflict that DEC-005 and DEC-006 settled in favour of the strict split policy.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-030..036 reports; outputs/DECISIONS.md (DEC-004 update, DEC-005, DEC-006)

\paragraph{Phase 4: continuous refinement and the flip audit (EXP-040--050, 104, 105).}
Residuals and Riemannian flows~\cite{chen2024rfm} improved synth precision slightly, flow-induced Tesseract masses did not help, and none of them fixed the real-domain errors. The flip audit (EXP-104) showed that these errors are $180^\circ$ body-axis flips caused by visual near-symmetry: the right mode is among the tree's hypotheses but is not selected. EXP-105 confirmed that no scorer on the clean features selects it on real images. An adaptive-depth oracle (EXP-050) showed small synth headroom (0.51$^\circ$), so adaptive depth became a low priority and was not pursued.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-040..045, EXP-050, EXP-104, EXP-105 reports; outputs/DECISIONS.md (DEC-010, DEC-012)

\paragraph{Real-domain phase (EXP-108--118).}
Four levers were proposed against mode selection. Augmentation plus orbit voting (EXP-109) cut the PoE errors from 26.91$^\circ$ / 36.66$^\circ$ to 12.31$^\circ$ / 12.28$^\circ$ (LB / SL), and the recipe was locked on it (DEC-018). Strengthening studies followed: tree vs flat (EXP-113), scoring rules (EXP-114), an augmentation ablation (EXP-115), two training-time alternatives to orbit voting (EXP-116, 117) and 6-DoF (EXP-118).
% src: outputs/DECISIONS.md (DEC-013 ... DEC-023); outputs/EXPERIMENT_INDEX.md (EXP-107..118 rows)

\paragraph{Cross-dataset phase (EXP-064, 106, 119, 122, 124, 126, 127).}
Per-object trees with the unchanged recipe worked on T-LESS and SYMSOL-T, failed on real-only YCB-V, and did not transfer zero-shot (EXP-064). Three fixes followed: symmetry-aware targets (EXP-106), synthetic YCB-V renders (EXP-122) and cross-dataset distillation (EXP-119). Matched comparisons with published work (EXP-124, 126) and the same-grid flat baseline (EXP-127) were added last. Along the way the subset-v1 feature caches of Phases 1--2 were deleted to free disk space (DEC-020), so those early runs cannot be re-evaluated without re-extracting features. Two incidents are worth knowing about. Nine SYMSOL-T MoGe-2 trees trained to NaN, because always-empty border cells had an sd of about $10^{-6}$ and overflowed fp16; they were retrained with an sd floor. A memory-guard false abort, caused by counting shared DataLoader pages per worker, stalled the EXP-124 queue for about 14.7\,h.

\subsection{Claim boundaries}\label{app:z_claims}

\paragraph{Claimed.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A lightweight hierarchical rotation predictor on frozen features: one Tesseract-path Transformer per backbone, fused per level by a training-free PoE, with about 4.5\,M trainable parameters. It is far more accurate than a count-matched flat Hopf classifier (EXP-113).
  \item Real-domain error on SPEED+ is a mode-selection problem: the large errors are $180^\circ$ flips whose correct mode is already among the hypotheses (EXP-104, 105).
  \item Photometric augmentation plus orbit voting resolves most flips. EXP-109 fixes 62\%/80\% of the $>150^\circ$ failures (LB/SL) and breaks 0.4\% of good predictions; the claim is orbit consistency, not a specific scoring rule.
  \item Orbit distillation improves both the single-view tree and the orbit system (EXP-117), as an enhancement.
  \item 6-DoF with the SPACE-HOP translation formulation, trained jointly without hurting rotation (EXP-118).
  \item With per-object retraining, the recipe transfers to T-LESS and SYMSOL-T, and multi-path targets (A2) improve both while keeping orbit voting effective (EXP-106).
\end{itemize}
% src: NOVELTY.md (Sec. 4 and updates 2026-10-17 to 2026-10-21); outputs/reports/EXP-104, EXP-105, EXP-109, EXP-113..118, EXP-106

\paragraph{Not claimed.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item No ``first'' claims: hierarchical $SO(3)$ grids, the recursive tesseract~\cite{kurz2017tesseract}, flow matching for pose, test-time voting and PoE all pre-exist.
  \item Not a better discretisation: per hypothesis the Tesseract is less precise than Hopf and Super-Fibonacci grids. Its head is faster only at large hypothesis counts (see Cost above).
  \item Hierarchy vs grid is open: the EXP-113 flat grid has a floor of 6.06$^\circ$ against 2.51$^\circ$, and the gain is not attributed to hierarchy until EXP-127 runs.
  \item No claim that the orbit product beats weighted voting (EXP-114), and no hierarchical-transport claim (EXP-116).
  \item The flow models are not calibrated, and the path flow has no geometry claim.
  \item Not training-free and not zero-shot: the trees are trained, and outside SPEED+ they are retrained per object.
  \item MoGe-2 fusion helps on SPEED+ and T-LESS only.
  \item External comparisons are unmatched: SPEED+ uses ground-truth crops (the box alone gives 0.56\,m translation error), with a synthetic-only detector the score rises to $0.523/0.333$ (detector IoU 0.76 on lightbox), and our T-LESS crops are not masked, unlike LiePose's~\cite{hsiao2024liepose}.
  \item Experiments never evaluated together are not combined (\eg the recipe with $w=0.8$).
\end{itemize}
