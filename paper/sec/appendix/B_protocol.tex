\subsection{SPEED+}\label{app:speedplus}

\paragraph{Source, splits and label checks.}
We use the original SPEED+ release~\cite{park2022speedplus}, read-only. Its four annotation files are \texttt{synthetic/train.json}, \texttt{synthetic/validation.json}, \texttt{lightbox/test.json} and \texttt{sunlamp/test.json}. The loader check (EXP-000) found no missing images, no image path shared between splits and unit-norm quaternions to within $10^{-6}$ (\cref{app:tab:spp_splits}). Quaternions are scalar-first (Hamilton), and the pose convention is $p_{\text{cam}}=R(q)\,p_{\text{body}}+r$ in the OpenCV camera frame ($x$ right, $y$ down, $z$ forward).
We confirmed this convention against its transpose by projecting the 11 Tango keypoints on 200 random images per domain. With $R(q)$, more of the projected keypoints land on bright spacecraft pixels (synthetic 0.482 vs.\ 0.261, lightbox 0.271 vs.\ 0.179, sunlamp 0.560 vs.\ 0.331). On synthetic images the keypoint box also matches an independent box table with mean IoU 0.970, against 0.519 for $R(q)^\top$. The quaternion--matrix round trip is exact to $2.96\times10^{-6}$ degrees.

\paragraph{Camera.}
Images are $1920\times1200$ grayscale with pixel pitch $5.86\,\mu$m. The intrinsics from \texttt{camera.json} are $f_x=2988.580$, $f_y=2988.340$ and $(c_x,c_y)=(960,600)$ px, with five distortion coefficients $(-0.22383, 0.51410, -0.00066, -0.00021, -0.13124)$. Distortion is applied whenever keypoints are projected, and it is removed when the viewing ray through a crop centre is computed for the in-plane roll (\cref{app:spp_aug}).

\paragraph{Rotation labels and the tree target.}
Each label is stored twice in the manifests: as the source quaternion (\texttt{q\_src\_*}) and as the sign-canonical quaternion of its tesseract chart (\texttt{q\_can\_*}). The manifests also store the chart, the 5-level child path, the depth-5 leaf id, the leaf centre and the quantisation error of that centre. EXP-001 checked the encoder invariants on all 69{,}491 labels. Over these labels, the leaf-centre quantisation error is $2.51^\circ$ on average (median $2.49^\circ$, maximum $5.78^\circ$). The analytic worst case over $SO(3)$ is $6.20^\circ$, which is the figure quoted in the main text.
% src: outputs/data_manifests/subset_full.csv (column quant_err_L5_deg: mean 2.5087, median 2.4873, max 5.7757); outputs/EXPERIMENT_INDEX.md (EXP-001, EXP-002)

\paragraph{Ground-truth square crops.}
The crop is built from the 11 Tango keypoints of the SPEED+ toolkit (\texttt{tangoPoints.mat}), projected with the ground-truth pose and the lens distortion. We take the tight box of the projected points, $[x_0,x_1]\times[y_0,y_1]$. The crop is the square centred on this box with side
\begin{equation*}
  s=\max(x_1-x_0,\;y_1-y_0,\;32\,\text{px})\cdot(1+2\cdot0.15),
\end{equation*}
\ie a 15\% margin per side, or $1.3\times$ the longer box edge. The square may extend beyond the image. It is resampled with an affine warp (bilinear, zero padding) to the backbone input size: $256\times256$ for DINOv3 ViT-L/16 (a $16\times16$ patch grid, ImageNet normalisation) and $518\times518$ for MoGe-2. The grayscale crop is replicated to three channels.
The crop side has a median of 724 px on synthetic images (range 309--2{,}570), 738 px on lightbox (339--2{,}286) and 738 px on sunlamp (332--2{,}117).
After the 2026-10-28 reboot, the keypoint file is read from a second copy of the toolkit. On 200 rows this copy reproduces every manifest crop to $<5\times10^{-6}$ px.

\paragraph{Training augmentation.}\label{app:spp_aug}
The backbones are frozen, so augmentation happens at the image level and features are re-extracted. Every synthetic-train crop has two pre-extracted augmented copies. At every training step each sample uses one of three views, drawn uniformly: the clean crop, copy 1 or copy 2.
Copy $j\in\{1,2\}$ uses seed $j$ in two ways. The roll angle of row $i$ is the $i$-th draw of \texttt{default\_rng(j).uniform(-180,180)} in manifest order. The photometric stream of row $i$ is \texttt{default\_rng([j,i])}. Both are fixed once and are identical for the two backbones.
Augmentation is applied to the $[0,1]$ crop in this order:
(1) brightness $\times\,\mathcal{U}(0.5,1.5)$;
(2) contrast about the mean, $\times\,\mathcal{U}(0.6,1.4)$;
(3) clip, then gamma $\mathcal{U}(0.6,1.6)$;
(4) with $p=0.5$, Gaussian blur with $\sigma\sim\mathcal{U}(0.01,1.5)$;
(5) with $p=0.3$, glare: 1--3 additive Gaussian blobs with amplitude $\mathcal{U}(0.5,1.5)$ and radius $\mathcal{U}(0.05,0.25)\cdot H$;
(6) always, Gaussian noise with $\sigma\sim\mathcal{U}(0,0.06)$, then clip;
(7) with $p=0.3$, random erasing of one rectangle covering 5--20\% of the area (aspect ratio $\mathcal{U}(0.3,3.3)$, uniform random fill);
(8) with $p=0.5$, JPEG re-encoding at quality 30--90.
The in-plane rotation by $\theta$ is applied in the crop warp itself. The rotated crop is sampled from the original image, so it has no black corners.
The label convention $q'=\Exp(-\theta d)\otimes q$ was derived from the camera geometry, with $d$ the undistorted unit ray through the crop centre. It was then verified on 512 synthetic-val rows at 4 angles. The keypoints pushed through the exact crop and rotation warp match the projection of $(q',r')$ to 0.34 px mean, 1.05 px p95 and 4.75 px maximum on a 224 px crop; the other three candidate conventions are off by 94--242 px. The label error measured by PnP is $0.06^\circ$ mean, $0.20^\circ$ p95 and $1.14^\circ$ maximum, and the round trip is within $0.004^\circ$.

\paragraph{Detector protocol (EXP-124).}\label{app:spp_det}
The detected-crop evaluation was pre-registered before the detector was trained and before any detected-crop result existed.
\emph{Data.} The detector label is the tight box of the 11 projected Tango keypoints, clipped to the image, with one class. This is the box the ground-truth crops are built from. Images are stored once at half resolution ($960\times600$, grayscale JPEG quality 95).
\emph{Training.} We use Ultralytics YOLO11s (COCO-pretrained), trained on synthetic/train only, with \texttt{imgsz} 960, 20 epochs, batch 32, seed 0 and deterministic mode. Augmentation is horizontal and vertical flips with $p=0.5$, no rotation or shear, mosaic (switched off for the last 5 epochs), brightness jitter \texttt{hsv\_v}=0.6 with no hue or saturation jitter, and the Ultralytics default albumentations. The checkpoint is the one with the best Ultralytics fitness on synthetic/validation, $0.1\,\text{mAP}_{50}+0.9\,\text{mAP}_{50:95}$.
\emph{Inference.} We keep the top-1 box at confidence $\ge0.001$, scale it to full resolution and turn it into a crop with the ground-truth rule (box centre; side $=\max(w,h,32)\times1.3$). When there is no detection, the whole image is used as the crop, and these cases are counted. Features of the detected crops and of their $90/180/270^\circ$ views are extracted with the same extractor and roll rule as the ground-truth test3 views.
\emph{Systems.} All systems were fixed before any result:
S1 (primary) uses the EXP-117 PoE students with orbit voting for rotation and the EXP-118 joint DINOv3 translation block with the orbit mean for translation;
S2 uses the EXP-118 joint PoE trees and blocks;
S3 uses the EXP-117 DINOv3 student with the same translation.
\emph{Reproduction gate.} A ground-truth-crop run with the same code must reproduce EXP-117 rotation ($11.21^\circ/11.09^\circ$) and EXP-118 translation ($0.185/0.183$\,m); otherwise the evaluator is considered wrong and nothing is reported. The ground-truth-crop run gives S1 $11.21^\circ/11.09^\circ$ and $0.185/0.183$\,m on lightbox/sunlamp, so the gate passes.
At the time of writing the detector training is still running, so no detection statistics are given here.

\paragraph{Official score.}
We use the SPEED+ score~\cite{park2022speedplus,park2023spec} as implemented by SPNv2~\cite{park2024spnv2}. Per image,
\begin{equation*}
  e_q=2\arccos|\langle\hat q,q\rangle|\ \text{(rad)},\qquad e_t=\frac{\|\hat t-t\|_2}{\|t\|_2},
\end{equation*}
and the score of a domain is the mean over its images of $e_q+e_t$. Each component is set to zero below the precision floor of the challenge: $e_q$ when the rotation error is below $0.169^\circ$, and $e_t$ when it is below $2.173\times10^{-3}$ (primary rule). As a check we also compute the joint rule, which zeroes the image score only when both components are below their floors. In the ground-truth-crop reference run the two rules differ by at most $1.1\times10^{-4}$ in any per-seed domain score.

\subsection{T-LESS}\label{app:tless}

\paragraph{Data and objects.}
We use the BOP release of T-LESS~\cite{hodan2017tless,hodan2020bop}, read-only, with RGB images. Intrinsics are stored per instance in the manifest: $f_x=1075.65$\,px throughout, with the principal point at $(360,270)$ for the PBR images and varying for the real ones. The translation target uses the $720\times540$ image size. The 7 objects were declared before any evaluation and span the symmetry classes in the BOP metadata (\cref{app:tab:tless}):
objects 1 and 4 have a continuous symmetry;
objects 5 and 11 have one discrete $180^\circ$ symmetry;
objects 18 and 21 have no symmetry in the metadata but are visually near-symmetric;
object 27 has three discrete symmetries.
Symmetries come from \texttt{models\_cad/models\_info.json} in the model frame, and the equivalent rotations are $R\,S$. Discrete symmetries are used exactly. Continuous ones are discretised in 36 steps of $10^\circ$ about their axis.
The full symmetry sets have sizes 36, 36, 2, 2, 1, 1 and 4 (identity included).

The crop is the ground-truth square box (margin 0.15 per side, minimum 32 px) around the 8 projected corners of the object's 3-D model bounding box. \paragraph{Augmentation and orbit views.}
T-LESS objects lie far from the optical axis, so the SPEED+-style affine rotation of the crop is not an exact camera roll. With it, the label error fails the pre-set rule: p95 $1.43^\circ$ and maximum $2.5^\circ$ on T-LESS, and p95 $2.40^\circ$ and maximum $6.7^\circ$ on YCB-V. Rolled views are therefore produced by the roll homography $K\,\mathrm{Rot}(d,-\theta)\,K^{-1}$ about the crop-centre ray. Labels transform exactly:
\begin{itemize}
  \item egocentric: $R'=\mathrm{Rot}(d,-\theta)R$ and $t'=\mathrm{Rot}(d,-\theta)t$;
  \item allocentric: the target is recomputed from $t'$.
\end{itemize}
The verified label error is $0.000^\circ$, and the mapping round trip is $\le0.004^\circ$.
The two augmented training copies use seeds 1 and 2, as on SPEED+, and the orbit views of validation and test rows are rolled by $90/180/270^\circ$ without photometric changes. On these allocentric datasets, mapping an orbit candidate between views needs the translation. We use the ground-truth translation, which is consistent with the ground-truth-crop protocol.

\paragraph{Training-target arms (EXP-106).}
All three arms are trained per object, with the same trainer, recipe, seeds and checkpoint rule.
\emph{A0} uses the raw annotated rotation; these are the EXP-064 trees.
\emph{A1} is canonical: in every view, the label is replaced by the equivalent $R\,S$ closest to the identity.
\emph{A2} is multi-path: the recipe's geodesic soft-target cross-entropy, averaged over the equivalents $R\,S$. All equivalents are used when $|\mathcal{S}|\le8$; otherwise 8 are drawn per row and step.
The pre-registration named the hard-path level-marginal loss of EXP-044. That loss would not reduce to A0 for objects without symmetry, which the pre-registration also requires, and it drops the soft targets. The smoothed loss was therefore adopted by an amendment written before any run. Symmetry composition was unit-checked beforehand: $q\,s$ matches $R\,S$ to $10^{-15}$, and the roll commutes with right-multiplication by $S$.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-106_tless_multipath_tree.md (Amendment 2026-10-27); scripts/train_xobj.py (--label, --sym-k 8, multi_loss, canon)

\paragraph{Differences from the LiePose protocol.}
The T-LESS comparison in \cref{tab:tless_sota} is not matched. LiePose~\cite{hsiao2024liepose} differs from our setting in four ways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item it fine-tunes its backbone jointly on all 30 objects, whereas we train frozen-feature trees per object;
  \item it multiplies the crop by the ground-truth visible mask, whereas we apply no mask;
  \item its per-object results (its Tab.~8) are averaged over our 7 objects for the comparison;
  \item its BOP-style symmetry handling discretises continuous symmetries more finely than our $10^\circ$ steps, so our symmetry-aware error for objects 1 and 4 can exceed the true symmetric error by up to $5^\circ$.
\end{enumerate}
We also use every BOP19 target with no visibility filter. A protocol on LiePose's own instance set (every ground-truth instance with visible area $>64$\,px, BOP-style fine discretisation) is reserved as EXP-125 and has not been run.

\paragraph{Symmetry handling and recall.}
For a predicted target rotation $\hat R$, the symmetry-aware error is
\begin{equation*}
  e_{\text{sym}}=\min_{S\in\mathcal{S}}\angle\big(\hat R,\;R\,S\big)=\arccos\Big(\tfrac{\max_S\operatorname{tr}(\hat R^\top R S)-1}{2}\Big),
\end{equation*}
computed in the allocentric target frame (re\_sym). The raw error (no minimum) is reported separately. On T-LESS it is $38$--$47^\circ$, which is why the symmetry-aware metric is needed. Recall at $k^\circ$ is the fraction of instances with $e_{\text{sym}}\le k$, for $k\in\{2,5,10,15,30\}$; the main text reports $k=2,5,10$.
For translation (EXP-126), the BOP symmetry transforms of our 7 objects have zero translation, so te\_sym equals $E_T$. Translation recall T@$c$ is the fraction with $E_T\le c$\,cm, for $c\in\{2,5,10\}$; T-LESS translations are in millimetres and are converted with a factor of 0.1.
% src: scripts/exp064_eval.py (sym_err, sym_acc@{2,5,10,15,30}, T@{2,5,10}cm with <=); src/tfpose/xobj.py (t_to_cm 0.1); outputs/reports/EXP-126_translation_xobj.md (Metrics); outputs/reports/EXP-064_broader_object_validation.md (raw means 38-47)

\subsection{SYMSOL-T}\label{app:symsolt}

\paragraph{Data.}
SYMSOL-T contains five symmetric solids (tetrahedron, cube, icosahedron, cone, cylinder) at random translations, following LiePose~\cite{hsiao2024liepose}. The local copy was rendered with LiePose's generator using seeds 12345 and 82973, so it follows the published protocol but is not guaranteed to be identical to the published image sets. We follow LiePose's protocol:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the input is the non-colour render, the full $224\times224$ frame, with no crop (crop side 224, margin 0);
  \item the target is the egocentric quaternion of the render;
  \item the error is the minimum over the annotated \texttt{rotations\_equivalent}.
\end{itemize}
The camera has a $60^\circ$ horizontal field of view, so the object origin is at $z=5.25/\tan30^\circ=9.09$ scene units and the focal length is $193.99$ px, with the principal point at the image centre. The in-plane rotation about the image centre is therefore an exact camera roll, with a verified label error of $0.000^\circ$.
% src: outputs/DECISIONS.md (DEC-009); scripts/exp064_symsolt_manifest.py (docstring); src/tfpose/xobj.py (SYM_Z, SYM_F); outputs/reports/EXP-064_broader_object_validation.md (Amendment 2026-10-18, item 1)

\paragraph{Split.}
From \texttt{train-45k}, 2{,}000 images per shape are held out with rng 0 for validation, and the remaining 43{,}000 per shape are training images. A pre-registered cap uses only the first 20{,}000 per shape, ordered by a seeded rank, for training, to fit the GPU budget. The test set is \texttt{test-5k}, 5{,}000 per shape. The manifest therefore has 25{,}000 test, 215{,}000 available training and 10{,}000 validation rows, of which 100{,}000 training rows are used.
% src: outputs/data_manifests/symsolt_exp064.csv and exp064_symsolt_all.csv (counts per split and shape); outputs/reports/EXP-064_broader_object_validation.md (Amendment 2026-10-18, item 2)

\paragraph{Symmetry groups.}
The groups recovered from the annotations, and checked to be constant across samples, have orders 12 (tetrahedron), 24 (cube) and 60 (icosahedron). The continuous groups are discretised by the annotations in $1^\circ$ steps: 360 elements for the cone and 720 for the cylinder, whose set also contains the flip.
These sets define the symmetry-aware error. They are also the equivalents of the multi-path arm A2, of which 8 are drawn per row and step for every shape here.
% src: outputs/data_manifests/symsolt_exp064_symmetries.npz (sizes; angles 0,1,2,... degrees for cone/cyl); scripts/exp064_symsolt_manifest.py; scripts/train_xobj.py (--sym-k 8)

\paragraph{Numerical fix.}
In the clean renders, some $16\times16$ MoGe-2 border cells are always empty, so their per-dimension training standard deviation is about $10^{-6}$. Rolled views place the object there, and the standardised values overflowed in half precision. As a result, the 9 MoGe-2 trees for the cube, cylinder and icosahedron diverged. These trees were retrained with a floor on the standard deviation, $\max(\mathrm{sd},10^{-2})$ (the features lie in $[-1,1]$), and the diverged runs are marked superseded. The same floor is used for these shapes in every later arm.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-064_broader_object_validation.md (SYMSOL-T first evaluation invalid); scripts/train_xobj.py (--sd-floor); outputs/reports/EXP-106_tless_multipath_tree.md (Amendment 2026-10-27)

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report the symmetry-aware rotation error (mean and median) and the recall at $2/5/10^\circ$ ($\le$), per shape and averaged over shapes. For translation (EXP-126) we report the mean Euclidean distance in scene units, the unit of LiePose's Tab.~3.

\paragraph{Diagnosis of the real-only failure (EXP-064).}
Trained on real videos only, the trees reach about $69^\circ$ on test. We ran four CPU checks to separate a pipeline bug from a generalisation failure:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The trees fit their training images: for example, $3.8^\circ$ (mustard) and $3.9^\circ$ (mug) mean error on training rows.
  \item Test poses are covered by training. For the cracker box, mustard and banana, the nearest training rotation is a median of about $5^\circ$ away, yet test errors are $75$--$87^\circ$.
  \item All of each object's label pixels lie inside its crop.
  \item The ground-truth rotations match the images: 65--98\% of the projected model points land on the object mask, against 28--83\% for a transposed-rotation control.
\end{enumerate}
We conclude that, with 14--20 cluttered training videos per object and unmasked tokens, the per-object trees memorise video context.

\subsection{Training details}\label{app:training}

\paragraph{Features and normalisation.}
Features are extracted once per view and cached.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{DINOv3:} last-layer patch tokens of ViT-L/16, average-pooled from $16\times16$ to $8\times8$ ($64\times1024$, stored in fp16).
  \item \emph{MoGe-2} (\texttt{normals16\textasciitilde and}): surface normals and mask pooled to $16\times16$ ($256\times4$). The consensus mask is the intersection of the MoGe-2 mask ($>0.5$) and a DINOv3 foreground, obtained by Otsu thresholding the first PCA component of the patch tokens.
\end{itemize}
Each feature dimension is standardised with the mean and standard deviation of the clean training rows, plus $10^{-6}$; the floor described in \cref{app:symsolt} applies where noted. The statistics are per dataset on SPEED+ and per object on the other benchmarks, and the same statistics are applied to augmented views, validation and test.
SPEED+ images are grayscale and replicated to three channels; T-LESS, SYMSOL-T and YCB-V are read as RGB.
% src: src/tfpose/featsets.py (tokens8, normals16, train_stats); scripts/extract_compact.py (consensus rule, --rgb); scripts/run_exp064.sh (--rgb); scripts/train_xobj.py (per-object stats)

\paragraph{Common recipe.}
All four trainers share one budget, the EXP-036 budget that was fixed at the Phase-3 gate (\cref{app:tab:train}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item 100 epochs with batch 256 and dropout 0.1;
  \item AdamW with learning rate $10^{-3}$ and weight decay 0.05;
  \item \texttt{OneCycleLR}, peak $10^{-3}$, 5\% warm-up, over $100\cdot\lceil N/256\rceil$ steps;
  \item bf16 autocast;
  \item validation every 5 epochs and at the last epoch;
  \item seeds 0, 1 and 2, set with \texttt{torch.manual\_seed} and \texttt{numpy.random.seed}.
\end{itemize}
In \texttt{train\_probe\_p3.py}, bf16 is the \texttt{--amp bf16} flag; the command line of every reported run shows it. The other three trainers always use bf16.
With augmentation, every sample draws one of its three views (clean, copy 1, copy 2) uniformly at every step.
The tree Transformer has a width of 256, one encoder layer and two causal decoder layers (4 heads, FFN 512), and a 6-frequency Fourier encoding of the cell centres. It has 2{,}391{,}564 parameters on DINOv3 tokens and 2{,}128{,}404 on MoGe-2 tokens.
The translation block is one learned query cross-attending (4 heads) to the encoded tokens, followed by $\text{LN}\to256\to\text{GELU}\to3$.

\subsection{Evaluation metrics}\label{app:metrics}

\paragraph{Rotation error.}
The geodesic error is $E_R=2\arccos|\langle\hat q,q\rangle|$ in degrees. On SPEED+ and YCB-V it is the raw error. On T-LESS and SYMSOL-T it is the symmetry-aware error of \cref{app:tless}. On T-LESS and YCB-V, $\hat q$ is compared with the ground truth in the allocentric target frame. Converting both to the egocentric frame with the ground-truth translation multiplies them by the same $R_{\text{ray}}(t)$, so the geodesic error is unchanged.
We report mean, median and p95.
Recall acc@$k$ is the fraction of images with error below $k^\circ$. On SPEED+, $k\in\{1,3,5,10,20\}$ with a strict $<$. On the other benchmarks, $k\in\{2,5,10,15,30\}$ with $\le$.
% src: src/tfpose/metrics.py (rotation_metrics, THRESH = (1, 3, 5, 10, 20), e < t); scripts/exp064_eval.py (sym_acc with e <= a); src/tfpose/xobj.py (target)

\paragraph{Severe errors and flips.}
\emph{Severe flips} are the fraction of images with $E_R>150^\circ$; we also report $E_R>90^\circ$.
\emph{Flip transitions} compare a system with a reference prediction on the same images. Let $n_{150}$ be the number of images with reference error $>150^\circ$, and $n_{20}$ the number with reference error $<20^\circ$. Then
\emph{corrected} $=\#\{e_0>150^\circ \wedge e<20^\circ\}/n_{150}$ and
\emph{broken} $=\#\{e_0<20^\circ \wedge e>150^\circ\}/n_{20}$.
The denominators are always reported. In the shared evaluator (EXP-113--115, 127), the reference is the greedy prediction of the same model family and variant. In EXP-109 it is the EXP-036 PoE greedy prediction, with $n_{150}=401$ (lightbox) and 206 (sunlamp).
% src: scripts/orbit_eval.py (frac_gt150_to_lt20, frac_lt20_to_gt150, n_greedy_gt150, n_greedy_lt20); outputs/reports/EXP-109_real_domain_aug_tokens16_orbit.md (flip table, of 401 / of 206)

\paragraph{Retention.}
For orbit distillation, the retention in each domain is
\begin{equation*}
  \rho=\frac{E^{\text{A}}_{\text{greedy}}-E^{\text{student}}_{\text{greedy}}}{E^{\text{A}}_{\text{greedy}}-E^{\text{A}}_{\text{orbit}}},
\end{equation*}
the share of the orbit-voting gain of the undistilled tree (A) that the single-view student recovers.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-117_orbit_distillation.md (Retention); outputs/reports/EXP-119_xobj_orbit_distillation.md

\paragraph{Translation.}
$E_T=\|\hat t-t\|_2$ is given in metres (SPEED+), millimetres (T-LESS), metres (YCB-V) or scene units (SYMSOL-T). We also report the normalised error $e_t=E_T/\|t\|_2$ and T@$c$\,cm $=\Pr[E_T\le c\,\text{cm}]$ for $c\in\{2,5,10\}$ (T-LESS, YCB-V).
The \emph{box-only} baseline predicts $U_x=U_y=0$ and $U_z$ equal to the training mean, mapped through the same inverse. It measures how much translation the ground-truth crop alone carries.
With orbit voting, each view's $\hat t$ is rolled back to view 0 and the four estimates are averaged.
% src: scripts/exp064_eval.py (E_T, e_t_norm, T@cm, box_only); src/tfpose/xobj.py (t_to_cm); outputs/reports/EXP-118_translation_6dof.md; outputs/reports/EXP-126_translation_xobj.md

\paragraph{Inference modes.}
\emph{Greedy} decodes view 0 only (beam 1).
\emph{Orbit product} collects beam-8 leaves from 4 views ($0/90/180/270^\circ$), maps them back to view 0, and scores each of the 32 candidates by the sum over views of the teacher-forced PoE path log-probability.
\emph{Weighted vote} (orbit\_vote32) scores each candidate by Gaussian-kernel support ($\sigma=5^\circ$) from all 32 candidates, weighted by their beam probabilities normalised within their view.
On allocentric datasets, candidates are mapped between views with the ground-truth translation (\cref{app:tless}).
% src: scripts/exp064_eval.py; scripts/exp124_eval.py; scripts/orbit_eval.py (docstring)

\paragraph{Aggregation.}
Errors are first averaged over the images of each object (or shape). Within a seed, these per-object means are averaged over objects with equal weight, which gives the object-balanced value. For a single object (SPEED+), this value is the plain mean over images. Medians and recalls are aggregated the same way.
The reported value is the mean of the per-seed object-balanced values over seeds 0--2. The $\pm$ value is their sample standard deviation (ddof $=1$) across the 3 seeds. Per-object tables average each object over the seeds.
% src: scripts/exp064_eval.py (per_object_seed -> object-balanced per seed -> mean/std over seeds; pandas std ddof=1); scripts/exp124_eval.py (per_seed -> mean/std)

\paragraph{Timing.}
End-to-end latency (EXP-112) is measured on real lightbox crops with the image already decoded. It covers the crop warps, backbones, tree encoding and beam search, and orbit scoring. It is measured at batch 1 and batch 64 as the median of 5 repetitions after warm-up, with CUDA synchronisation. The concurrent GPU load is recorded; the GPU is shared, so all timings are upper bounds.
% src: outputs/reports/EXP-112_locked_recipe_eval_timing.md (Timing protocol)

\subsection{Selection and pre-registration protocol}\label{app:prereg}

\paragraph{Split policy.}
We fixed the split policy at the start of the project (DEC-000) and restored it in DEC-006:
\begin{itemize}
  \item train on SPEED+ synthetic/train only;
  \item select and gate on synthetic/validation only;
  \item treat all 6{,}740 lightbox and 2{,}791 sunlamp images as test-only and descriptive.
\end{itemize}
DEC-005 had briefly allowed a seeded 20\% lightbox validation split (1{,}348 images). DEC-006 withdrew it on the same day, before it influenced any training run or checkpoint, and rewrote the one gate that referred to it before any result.
The same policy carries over to the other benchmarks: train on the permitted training split, select on a held-out validation split built by whole scenes or sequences, and use the test split for reporting only.
Synthetic validation contains almost no gross errors, so from EXP-109 on SPEED+ selection also uses the synthetic val-aug proxy (\cref{app:speedplus}).

\end{itemize}
Both variants (PoE and DINOv3-only) are reported everywhere, with and without orbit voting. The following are explicitly not part of the recipe: 16$\times$16 tokens, the weighted PoE, discrete flow matching, flow-teacher distillation and the corner tree.

\paragraph{Pre-registration and the pass rule.}
Each experiment has a report in \texttt{outputs/reports/}. Before any run, the report records:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the question;
  \item the arms and controls;
  \item the selection data;
  \item the metrics;
  \item the pass criterion;
  \item for the main experiments, the expected outcome.
\end{itemize}
The standard pass rule compares 3-seed means on validation data: a difference passes if it exceeds the larger of the two seed standard deviations. Some criteria add a non-inferiority clause on clean synthetic validation, using the same tolerance.
Real-domain and test numbers are reported for every arm, whatever their direction, and are never used for selection.

\paragraph{Amendments and deviations.}
A protocol change goes into the report as a dated ``Amendment'' section, written before the affected runs and stating what changes and why. Earlier text is not edited. Deviations found after the fact are recorded as such, with the run identifiers. Examples:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{EXP-109.} The predictor-based convention gate was replaced, after an external review and before any augmented run, by a geometric derivation. A pixel threshold chosen before measuring was then replaced, before the degree measurement was taken, by a label-error rule; both runs are kept.
  \item \emph{Compact-feature gate (EXP-064).} The gate, which compares the compact cache with the full features, was pre-registered to run before use but ran after the T-LESS results. Its strict tolerance fails (MoGe-2 maximum deviation 0.51 standard deviations, p99 0.004). The pre-set functional follow-up shows no impact: on 512 crops no PoE greedy prediction changes, and 1 and 3 single-branch leaves change. Both are on record.
  \item \emph{EXP-106.} The multi-path loss was changed to the smoothed version (\cref{app:tless}), and the arm K and best-fit stages were left unrun, by an amendment written before any run.
  \item \emph{SYMSOL-T.} The NaN fix (\cref{app:symsolt}) was recorded as a bug fix, not a recipe change; only the affected trees were retrained.
  \item \emph{EXP-064 zero-shot arm.} The pre-set rule was met literally on T-LESS. A post-hoc constant-prediction control, labelled as post hoc, shows that this comes from the pose prior, so no transfer is claimed.
  \item \emph{EXP-127.} An amendment written before any trained-arm run defines per-system inference memory and adds a gate: the tree must reproduce EXP-113 seed 0 to within $0.02^\circ$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Compute and infrastructure}\label{app:compute}

\paragraph{Hardware and software.}
Every logged run that records its GPU (740 runs) used one NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada Generation, in a workstation shared with other users, and Python 3.13.11. The PyTorch build differs by period. Runs of EXP-000 to EXP-042, EXP-101 and EXP-104 (and some smoke tests) used PyTorch 2.11.0 with CUDA 13.0. Runs from EXP-043 on, including every run from EXP-109 onward, used PyTorch 2.8.0 with CUDA 12.8. All runs used cuDNN 9.19. The locked-recipe runs also record timm 1.0.27 and OpenCV 5.0.0. Each run directory stores its own environment, so per-run versions can be checked.
% src: grep of gpu/torch/python/cuda lines over outputs/experiments/*/RUN-*/environment.txt (gpu 740x RTX 6000 Ada; torch 2.8.0+cu128 x493, 2.11.0+cu130 x255 in EXP-000..042, 099, 101, 104); outputs/experiments/EXP-109_real_domain_aug_tokens16_orbit/RUN-20261015-180909-seed0/environment.txt

\paragraph{Memory guards.}
Every heavy job runs through \texttt{scripts/guarded.sh}, which provides four protections:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a hard kernel cap: a \texttt{systemd-run} scope with \texttt{MemoryMax} (40\,GB by default, set per job), no swap, and at most 4{,}096 tasks;
  \item admission control: a job starts only when the machine has the job's cap plus 12\,GB of RAM available and, by default, 8\,GB of free GPU memory;
  \item low priority: the job runs at \texttt{nice} 10 and idle I/O priority;
  \item maintenance pause: new jobs wait while \texttt{outputs/experiments/QUEUE\_PAUSE} exists, while running jobs are untouched.
\end{itemize}
Inside each job, \texttt{tfpose.memguard} acts as a soft guard. Every 0.25\,s it polls the process's own RSS plus the USS of its child processes, and the system's available memory. It aborts the job, writes the reason into the run record and kills the job's children before the kernel cap is reached. Its limit is 85\% of the cap, and it also aborts if available memory falls below 16\,GB.
Children were first counted by RSS, which counts pages shared with forked DataLoader workers once per worker. During EXP-124 this produced a false abort, reporting 44.3\,GB against a real peak of 11\,GB. The orphaned workers then stalled the queue for about 14.7\,h. Children have been counted by USS since then.
A watchdog checks the queues every 5 minutes. It kills orphaned DataLoader workers, marks runs whose process has died as failed, and raises alerts for dead or stuck queues, idle jobs and low memory.
% src: scripts/guarded.sh; src/tfpose/memguard.py (interval_s 0.25, DEFAULT_MIN_AVAIL_GB 16, _rss_gb); scripts/watchdog.py (docstring); outputs/reports/EXP-124_speedplus_detected_official_score.md (Incident)

\paragraph{Feature caches.}
Features are extracted once per view and stored on an 8\,TB drive (DEC-020): the full SPEED+ caches (105\,GB when moved) and the compact caches, about 170\,GB for the three EXP-064 datasets. Each cache directory is named by backbone, subset tag, preprocessing hash and manifest hash, and stores its specification, git commit, timing and peak GPU memory.
On the full SPEED+ split, extraction took 11.5\,ms per image for DINOv3 (peak 1.6\,GB GPU) and 29.2\,ms per image for MoGe-2 (peak 3.0\,GB).
The compact extractor stores only what the recipe needs: \texttt{tokens8}, the DINOv3 foreground, and the MoGe-2 normals and mask. It was checked against the full caches as described above.

\paragraph{Run records.}
Every training and evaluation script opens a \texttt{tfpose.runlog.Run}. It claims a directory \path{outputs/experiments/EXP-nnn_<name>/RUN-<timestamp>-seed<s>} and writes the following files:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{config.yaml}: all arguments;
  \item \texttt{command.txt}: the exact command;
  \item \texttt{environment.txt}: host, library versions, GPU, git commit and a flag for uncommitted code changes;
  \item \texttt{status.json}: state, PID, start and end, \texttt{wall\_clock\_s}, peak CPU RSS, peak GPU memory, and for trainings the selected epoch and parameter count.
\end{itemize}
Metrics are written as CSV, JSON and TSV, and checkpoints, logs and predictions go to subfolders. Runs that fail or are superseded keep their directories, with the error or the reason in \texttt{status.json}.
