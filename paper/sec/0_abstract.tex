\begin{abstract}
Single-view 6D object pose estimation often fails not because the correct pose cannot be represented, but because it is not \emph{selected}: symmetric objects have several equally correct rotations, and near-symmetric ones have look-alike rotations that are wrong. We study this selection problem with a deliberately lightweight design. Two frozen foundation backbones (DINOv3 for appearance, MoGe-2 for surface geometry) feed small autoregressive Transformers that predict a rotation as a root-to-leaf path in a hierarchical tesseract partition of $SO(3)$. The two branches are fused per tree level by a training-free product of experts, and a translation block on the same tokens completes the 6D pose.
Three components address selection: (i) \emph{symmetry-aware multi-path targets} that spread the training signal over all equivalent rotations; (ii) \emph{orbit voting}, which scores hierarchical hypotheses for consistency across in-plane rotations of the crop through an exactly derived camera-roll transform; and (iii) \emph{orbit distillation} of that consistency into single-view students.
We evaluate on T-LESS, SYMSOL-T, YCB-V and the synthetic-to-real benchmark SPEED+, training only 2.4\,M parameters per branch. Multi-path targets reduce the SYMSOL-T error from $3.82^\circ$ to $2.07^\circ$. On SPEED+, orbit voting and distillation reduce the real-domain error from $26.9^\circ/36.7^\circ$ to $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$. With synthetic training data, YCB-V drops from $81^\circ$ to $10.9^\circ$. Absolute accuracy remains below fine-tuned state-of-the-art methods, and we report matched comparisons, pre-registered ablations and negative results throughout.
\end{abstract}
