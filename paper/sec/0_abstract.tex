\begin{abstract}
We study 6D pose estimation of known objects with a deliberately lightweight design: one frozen foundation backbone and one core algorithm are shared by every application, and only a small downstream head is trained per application. Two frozen backbones (DINOv3 for appearance, MoGe-2 for surface geometry) feed small autoregressive Transformers (2.4\,M parameters per branch) that predict a rotation as a root-to-leaf path in a hierarchical tesseract partition of $SO(3)$. The branches are fused per tree level by a training-free product of experts, and a translation block on the same tokens completes the 6D pose.
Single-view pose often fails not because the correct pose cannot be represented, but because it is not \emph{selected}: symmetric objects have several equally correct rotations, and nearly symmetric ones have look-alike rotations that are wrong. Three components address selection: (i) \emph{symmetry-aware multi-path targets} that spread the training signal over all equivalent rotations; (ii) \emph{orbit voting}, which scores hierarchical hypotheses for consistency across in-plane rotations of the crop through an exactly derived camera-roll transform; and (iii) \emph{orbit distillation} of that consistency into single-view students.
On general objects, multi-path targets improve T-LESS at every recall threshold (orbit R@10 $73.5\%\to79.0\%$) and every SYMSOL-T shape (mean $2.62^\circ\to2.07^\circ$), and synthetic training data reduces the validation-selected YCB-V error from $69^\circ$ to $12.9^\circ$. As an application under a hard synthetic-to-real shift, the same design estimates spacecraft pose on SPEED+, where orbit voting and distillation reduce the real-domain error from $26.9^\circ/36.7^\circ$ to $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$. Absolute accuracy remains below fine-tuned state-of-the-art methods. We report matched comparisons, pre-registered ablations, negative results and the deployment cost of the downstream head throughout.
\end{abstract}
