\begin{abstract}
Adapting a 6D pose estimator to a new object or application usually means fine-tuning a large network on new data. We study whether shared, \emph{frozen} foundation backbones can instead support accurate 6D pose of known objects across very different object types, with only small downstream heads trained per object. Frozen DINOv3 appearance features, optionally fused with frozen MoGe-2 surface-normal features, feed a compact head (2.4\,M parameters per branch) that predicts rotation as a root-to-leaf path in a hierarchical tesseract partition of $SO(3)$, and a 0.33\,M-parameter block that predicts translation from the same encoded tokens. A base head trains in a median of 5--16 minutes from cached features (distilled students take 40--84 minutes); the backbones (0.30\,B and 0.33\,B parameters) are never trained.
Because single-view errors are dominated by selecting the wrong mode among symmetric or look-alike rotations, the heads use symmetry-aware multi-path supervision, orbit aggregation of hierarchical hypotheses over exactly mapped in-plane rotations, and optional distillation of that consistency into single-view students.
With validation-selected configurations and ground-truth crops, the same architecture reaches a recall of 79.0\% at $10^\circ$ and 88.0\% at 5\,cm on real T-LESS images, $2.07^\circ$ on SYMSOL-T, and $12.9^\circ$ and 1.6\,cm on YCB-V, where training on real videos alone fails; on T-LESS and YCB-V these rotation results use the ground-truth translation to convert allocentric predictions. Applied unchanged to spacecraft pose under a synthetic-to-real shift (SPEED+), it reaches $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$ on the real domains; with a synthetic-only detector instead of ground-truth crops the error doubles on one of them. Multi-path targets improve every symmetric benchmark and, unlike canonical labels, keep orbit aggregation effective; orbit aggregation helps on three of the four benchmarks but not on cluttered YCB-V; geometry fusion helps only on two. Fine-tuned keypoint methods remain 3--10$\times$ more accurate, but they back-propagate through their backbones about 1--7 million times per target; without any backbone gradient, our heads match or exceed fine-tuned direct regressors and close 30--58\% of the gap between a frozen-feature baseline and LoRA fine-tuning.
\end{abstract}
