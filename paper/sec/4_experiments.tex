\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Benchmarks and protocol}
\label{sec:setup}
\paragraph{Benchmarks.} We use three general-object benchmarks that stress different kinds of ambiguity, and one downstream application. Every benchmark uses the same frozen backbones, the same head architecture, training recipe and inference algorithm; only the heads are trained.
\textbf{T-LESS}~\cite{hodan2017tless}: textureless industrial parts with continuous, discrete or no symmetries. We use 7 objects spanning these classes (1, 4, 5, 11, 18, 21, 27), train on PBR renders and real training images, select on held-out PBR scenes, and test on all real BOP19 targets~\cite{hodan2020bop} of these objects.
\textbf{SYMSOL-T}: five symmetric solids (tetrahedron, cube, icosahedron, cone, cylinder) with random translations, rendered with LiePose's generator and seeds~\cite{hsiao2024liepose} (so the images follow its protocol but are not guaranteed identical to its published sets).
\textbf{YCB-V}~\cite{xiang2018posecnn}: household objects in cluttered real videos. We use 6 objects without declared symmetries (cracker box, mustard bottle, banana, mug, power drill, scissors), validate on held-out real sequences and test on the real keyframes.
\textbf{Application: SPEED+}~\cite{park2022speedplus}: spacecraft pose estimation for autonomous rendezvous, with a nearly symmetric target and a hard synthetic-to-real shift: 47,966 synthetic training and 11,994 synthetic validation images, tested on two real domains, \emph{lightbox} (6,740) and \emph{sunlamp} (2,791). Training uses synthetic images only.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Experiments followed pre-registered protocols: question, arms, controls and pass criterion were fixed before each run, and protocol changes were recorded before the affected runs. Post-hoc analyses are labelled as such. We train 3 seeds and report mean $\pm$ seed standard deviation; a criterion passes if the difference exceeds the larger seed standard deviation. Checkpoints, hyper-parameters and variants are selected on validation data only; real test sets are never used for selection. One tree per object and branch is trained with the same recipe on every dataset. Two inference options are chosen per application on its validation data, never on test data: the variant (PoE or DINOv3-only) and whether to use orbit voting. Errors are averaged per object and then over objects. Unless stated otherwise, crops come from ground-truth boxes (\cref{sec:limitations}). Failed criteria and negative results are reported (\cref{sec:supp_negative}).

\paragraph{Implementation.} Trees are trained for 100 epochs with batch 256, AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay 0.05, one-cycle schedule) and bf16, which takes about 3--20 minutes per tree on one shared GPU (about 13 minutes for a DINOv3 tree under the locked SPEED+ recipe; \cref{app:protocol}). Timings are measured end-to-end at batch 1 on a shared NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada and are upper bounds.

\paragraph{T-LESS (\cref{tab:tless_sota}).} Orbit voting raises recall at all thresholds for the single-label trees (R@10 $68.1\to73.5\%$), and canonical labels help the single view but break orbit voting (\cref{sec:abl_sym}). Multi-path targets (A2) are best at every threshold, with or without orbit voting: with orbit voting, R@2/5/10 rise to $5.7/48.1/79.0\%$ and the mean error falls to $15.2^\circ$, and on validation the orbit error drops from $9.25^\circ$ (A0) to $8.28^\circ$, which passes the pre-registered criterion. Unlike canonical labels, multi-path targets keep orbit voting effective. LiePose is clearly more accurate, especially at fine thresholds (R@2 $34.9\%$ vs.\ $5.7\%$). Three factors are confounded in this gap: LiePose fine-tunes its backbone on all 30 objects, it masks the crop with the ground-truth visible mask, and our depth-5 leaves quantise rotations to $2.5^\circ$ on average, which caps R@2.

\paragraph{SYMSOL-T (\cref{tab:symsol_sota}).} Multi-path targets lower the error on every shape, from $2.62^\circ$ to $2.07^\circ$ on average. For symmetric shapes this is below the $2.31^\circ$ error of the ground-truth rotation's own leaf, because the tree may pick the leaf closest to \emph{any} equivalent rotation. LiePose remains about $4\times$ more accurate; closing the gap needs a continuous refinement on top of the selected leaf.

\paragraph{YCB-V (\cref{tab:ycbv}).} Trained on the real training videos alone, the trees fit their training frames but memorise video context and fail on new sequences (test $69$--$81^\circ$; \cref{sec:supp_ycbv}). Adding the standard synthetic renders, pasted onto real training-sequence backgrounds, turns this into a working model. The configuration selected on validation (DINOv3, single view, $9.0^\circ$ validation) reaches $12.9^\circ$ on the real test keyframes, with 85\% of predictions within $10^\circ$; the PoE single-view model is better on test ($10.9^\circ$, 87\%) but is not the validation choice. Five of the six objects reach $4$--$8^\circ$, and the thin, near-planar scissors remain the main failure (\cref{tab:ycbv_perobj}). Unlike on the other benchmarks, orbit voting does \emph{not} help on YCB-V: it raises the validation error from $9.0^\circ$ to $11.5^\circ$ (DINOv3) and from $10.5^\circ$ to $12.7^\circ$ (PoE). We have not yet diagnosed why; one hypothesis is that, with cluttered real backgrounds and a narrow range of camera roll in the training videos, the rotated views are scored less reliably than the original view (\cref{app:results}). Published YCB-V results use BOP or ADD(-S) metrics on all 21 objects with detected boxes, so we do not place our numbers in those tables.

\paragraph{Translation (\cref{tab:trans_xobj}).} The translation block is the same on every dataset (\cref{sec:sixdof_method}) and is trained on the frozen tree selected for rotation, using each dataset's own image size. Its evaluation on T-LESS, SYMSOL-T and YCB-V was pre-registered together with these rotation results. On YCB-V the block passes its criterion clearly: on validation, the orbit-averaged error is $3.4$\,cm against $10.7$\,cm for the box alone. On the real test keyframes, the single-view block places 73\% of the objects within 2\,cm and 96\% within 5\,cm (mean $1.6$\,cm, against $8.1$\,cm for the box alone). As for rotation, orbit averaging does not help on YCB-V ($1.8$\,cm), and validation selects the single view. T-LESS and SYMSOL-T are still running. On the spacecraft application it is already evaluated (\cref{sec:sixdof}).

\subsection{Application: spacecraft pose under synthetic-to-real shift}
\label{sec:spp}
We now apply the same design, unchanged, to spacecraft pose estimation for autonomous rendezvous. Only renders of the spacecraft are available for training, and the test images come from two hardware-in-the-loop facilities. The target is nearly symmetric, so this application stresses exactly the look-alike failure mode that orbit voting targets.

\paragraph{Comparison with published results (\cref{tab:spp_sota}).} With ground-truth crops, our frozen-feature system reaches $11.2^\circ/11.1^\circ$, close to recent direct methods (PAID-ViT $12.3^\circ/11.8^\circ$) and well ahead of a head on frozen DINOv3 features in the same crop setting ($15.5^\circ/24.5^\circ$~\cite{church2026vfm}). Fine-tuned keypoint pipelines are far more accurate ($1.2^\circ$--$3.4^\circ$). Our official score with ground-truth crops is $0.227/0.224$ for the distilled system (rotation from the distilled PoE students with orbit voting, translation from the jointly trained DINOv3 block) and $0.241/0.238$ for the jointly trained 6-DoF PoE model. This is similar to SPNv2 without adaptation on sunlamp and to PAID-ViT, and it is dominated by the rotation term.
With boxes from a detector trained on synthetic images only (YOLO11s; a missed detection falls back to the full image and is still scored), the same system reaches $23.9^\circ/16.4^\circ$ and a score of $0.523/0.333$. The detector is reliable on synthetic images (mean IoU 0.99) and on sunlamp (0.88, no misses) but not on lightbox (mean IoU 0.76, 16\% of boxes below IoU 0.5, 54 misses), so the lightbox gap is mainly a detection problem. Translation suffers most, because our translation target is defined relative to the box ($0.66/0.27$\,m against $0.19/0.18$\,m with ground-truth boxes). This matched, synthetic-only setting is far behind SPNv2 without adaptation ($0.142/0.230$), which predicts from the full image.


\paragraph{Diagnosis: real-domain errors are flips.}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
Before changing the model, we audited the large real-domain errors of the clean (non-augmented) SPEED+ trees. Encoder and decoder round trips are exact, and the metric, rotation convention and data pipeline were ruled out. Chart seams were ruled out as well: flat Hopf-grid heads on the same features flip as often or more. Of the errors above $90^\circ$, 36--41\% lie within $15^\circ$ of $180^\circ$, against 20\% for uniformly random wrong rotations. Their body-frame rotation axes cluster around a few axes of the near-symmetric spacecraft. Allowing a $180^\circ$ rotation about one body axis lowers the lightbox mean of the DINOv3 tree from $38.9^\circ$ to $23.5^\circ$.
The correct mode is usually available: among the tree's beam candidates and their $180^\circ$ flips, an oracle achieves $9.8^\circ$ on lightbox, against $26.9^\circ$ for the fused greedy prediction. Neither a continuous flow posterior, flow-based re-noise scoring, synthetic templates nor a discrete path flow selects it on real images (\cref{sec:supp_negative}). The problem is \emph{mode selection} under domain shift.

\paragraph{Orbit voting and distillation.} \Cref{tab:main} summarises the progression. Augmentation alone (photometric plus roll, single view) cuts the real-domain error of the PoE system from $26.9^\circ/36.7^\circ$ to $17.3^\circ/20.2^\circ$. Orbit voting then brings it to $12.3^\circ/12.3^\circ$ and the synthetic error to $3.87^\circ$. It repairs 62\% and 80\% of the severe flips on lightbox and sunlamp while breaking only 0.4\% of good predictions (\cref{tab:flips}). The median errors on real images are $5.3^\circ$ and $6.3^\circ$, so the remaining means are driven by a tail of failures.
Orbit distillation improves every setting. The single-view students recover 13--43\% of the orbit gain at single-view cost; for example, the PoE greedy error on lightbox drops from $17.3^\circ$ to $15.9^\circ$. Students with orbit voting give the best system: $3.69^\circ$ synthetic and $11.21^\circ/11.09^\circ$ on lightbox/sunlamp, about twice the seed std better than the undistilled orbit system, with fewer severe flips. For distillation, synthetic validation ranks the systems in the same order. For the orbit \emph{scoring rule} it does not: the likelihood product was fixed in the base recipe before the scoring ablation was run, and synthetic validation would have selected the weighted vote instead (\cref{sec:ablations}), which gives $3.27^\circ/11.35^\circ/11.91^\circ$ with the distilled PoE students.
MoGe-2 helps on real images: PoE with orbit voting reaches $12.3^\circ$ on lightbox, against $17.4^\circ$ for DINOv3-only. It costs about 50\,ms per view, half of which is the CPU consensus-mask step. DINOv3-only orbit voting runs at 14\,FPS and a single DINOv3 view at 68\,FPS (\cref{sec:supp_timing}).

\paragraph{Full 6-DoF.}
\label{sec:sixdof}
\Cref{tab:sixdof} (supplementary) adds translation with the SPACE-HOP target~\cite{dutta2026spacehop}. Because our crops come from ground-truth boxes, the box alone already locates the target to $0.55$\,m. The translation block improves on this by about $7$--$10\times$ on synthetic data (both pre-registered criteria pass). Joint training does not hurt rotation and even helps slightly on real images: PoE + orbit reaches $3.86^\circ/12.11^\circ/11.73^\circ$, against $3.87^\circ/12.30^\circ/12.25^\circ$. Orbit averaging lowers translation error by 10--25\%. The DINOv3 block is the most accurate on synthetic data ($0.059$\,m) and reaches $0.185/0.183$\,m on the real domains; the MoGe-2 block alone is weak for translation. With a synthetic-only detector instead of ground-truth boxes, translation error rises to $0.66/0.27$\,m (last row of \cref{tab:spp_sota}).
