\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Setup and protocol}
\paragraph{Data.} SPEED+~\cite{park2022speedplus} provides 47,966 synthetic training images, 11,994 synthetic validation images, and two real test domains: \emph{lightbox} (6,740) and \emph{sunlamp} (2,791). We train on synthetic training images only.

\paragraph{Protocol.} Every experiment was pre-registered before it was run: its question, arms, controls and pass criterion were fixed in advance, and protocol changes were recorded before the affected runs. We train 3 seeds and report mean $\pm$ seed standard deviation. A criterion ``passes'' if the difference exceeds the larger seed standard deviation. Checkpoints, hyper-parameters and variants are selected on synthetic validation only; lightbox and sunlamp are used only for testing. Some ablations also report a \emph{rotated validation} proxy, the synthetic validation set with random in-plane rolls. Failed criteria and negative results are reported (\cref{sec:supp_negative}).

\paragraph{Implementation.} Trees are trained for 100 epochs with batch 256, AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay 0.05, one-cycle schedule) and bf16, which takes about 13 minutes per DINOv3 tree on one GPU. Timings are measured end-to-end at batch 1 on a shared NVIDIA RTX 6000 Ada; other jobs were running on it, so they are upper bounds. All evaluations use ground-truth crops (\cref{sec:limitations}).

\subsection{Diagnosis: real-domain errors are flips}
\label{sec:diagnosis}
Before changing the model, we audited the large real-domain errors of the clean (non-augmented) trees. Encoder and decoder round trips are exact, and the metric, rotation convention and data pipeline were ruled out. Chart seams were ruled out as well: flat Hopf-grid heads on the same features flip as often or more. Of the errors above $90^\circ$, 36--41\% lie within $15^\circ$ of $180^\circ$, against 20\% for uniformly random wrong rotations. Their body-frame rotation axes cluster around a few axes of the near-symmetric spacecraft body. Allowing a $180^\circ$ rotation about one body axis lowers the lightbox mean of the DINOv3 tree from $38.9^\circ$ to $23.5^\circ$.
The correct mode is usually available: among the tree's beam candidates and their $180^\circ$ flips, an oracle achieves $9.8^\circ$ on lightbox, against $26.9^\circ$ for the fused greedy prediction. Neither a continuous flow posterior, flow-based re-noise scoring, synthetic templates nor a discrete path flow selects it on real images (\cref{sec:supp_negative}). The problem is \emph{mode selection} under domain shift.

\Cref{tab:main} summarises the progression. Augmentation alone (photometric plus roll, single view) cuts the real-domain error of the PoE system from $26.9^\circ/36.7^\circ$ to $17.3^\circ/20.2^\circ$. Orbit voting then brings it to $12.3^\circ/12.3^\circ$ and the synthetic error to $3.87^\circ$. It repairs 62\% and 80\% of the severe flips on lightbox and sunlamp while breaking only 0.4\% of good predictions (\cref{tab:flips}). The median errors on real images are $5.3^\circ$ and $6.3^\circ$, so the remaining means are driven by a tail of failures.
Orbit distillation improves every setting. The single-view students recover 13--43\% of the orbit gain at single-view cost; for example, the PoE greedy error on lightbox drops from $17.3^\circ$ to $15.9^\circ$. Students with orbit voting give the best system: $3.69^\circ$ synthetic and $11.21^\circ/11.09^\circ$ on lightbox/sunlamp, about twice the seed std better than the undistilled orbit system, with fewer severe flips. Synthetic validation, the only selection signal, ranks the systems in the same order.
MoGe-2 helps on real images: PoE with orbit voting reaches $12.3^\circ$ on lightbox, against $17.4^\circ$ for DINOv3-only. It costs about 50\,ms per view, half of which is the CPU consensus-mask step. DINOv3-only orbit voting runs at 14\,FPS and a single DINOv3 view at 68\,FPS (\cref{sec:supp_timing}).

\paragraph{Context.} Published SPEED+ results are not directly comparable. Most use detected boxes, full 6-DoF pipelines with PnP or refinement, and fine-tuned networks, whereas we use ground-truth crops and frozen backbones. As reported, keypoint/PnP methods with domain adaptation reach lower real-domain rotation errors (SPNv2: $9.8^\circ/5.6^\circ$; Pérez-Villar~\etal: $2.7^\circ/4.6^\circ$ on lightbox/sunlamp~\cite{dutta2026spacehop}). Direct regressors are closer to us: SPACE-HOP reports $9.0^\circ/26.6^\circ$ and FastPose-ViT $28.0^\circ/14.7^\circ$~\cite{dutta2026spacehop,ancey2026fastpose}. Our contribution is a light frozen-feature design and an analysis of what makes it transfer, not a new state of the art.

\Cref{tab:sixdof} reports the 6-DoF results. Because our crops come from ground-truth boxes, the box alone already locates the target to $0.55$\,m. The translation block improves on this by about $7$--$10\times$ on synthetic data (both pre-registered criteria pass). Joint training does not hurt rotation and even helps slightly on real images: PoE + orbit reaches $3.86^\circ/12.11^\circ/11.73^\circ$, against $3.87^\circ/12.30^\circ/12.25^\circ$. Orbit averaging lowers translation error by 10--25\%. The DINOv3 block is the most accurate on synthetic data ($0.059$\,m), and PoE averaging is best on lightbox ($0.175$\,m). The MoGe-2 block alone is weak for translation.
SPACE-HOP reports $0.096/0.302/0.282$\,m (synthetic/lightbox/sunlamp)~\cite{dutta2026spacehop}. Our numbers are lower, but they rely on ground-truth boxes, use different crop padding and average over 4 views, so the comparison is not matched.

\label{sec:xds}

One tree is trained per object and branch with the unchanged recipe. T-LESS: synthetic PBR validation, real BOP test. SYMSOL-T: LiePose split. YCB-V: held-out real sequences and keyframes.}
  \label{tab:xds}

We apply the unchanged recipe to three datasets with per-object trees (\cref{tab:xds}).
For \textbf{T-LESS}~\cite{hodan2017tless} we use 7 objects with continuous, discrete or no symmetries, trained on PBR renders and real training images, and tested on the real BOP test targets~\cite{hodan2020bop}. For \textbf{SYMSOL-T} we use the five shapes with the split of~\cite{hsiao2024liepose}. For \textbf{YCB-V}~\cite{xiang2018posecnn} we use 6 objects without symmetry metadata, trained on real training videos only.
Labels follow \cref{eq:roll}, using the exact roll homography on T-LESS and YCB-V.

\emph{Orbit voting transfers wherever the base model generalises.} It reduces error on T-LESS ($12.3^\circ\!\to\!9.3^\circ$ val, $17.9^\circ\!\to\!16.6^\circ$ real test) and on SYMSOL-T ($3.8^\circ\!\to\!2.6^\circ$, with 100\% of test predictions within $10^\circ$).
\emph{PoE fusion is not general.} It passes its pre-registered criterion on T-LESS, but on the textureless SYMSOL-T renders MoGe-2 adds nothing, and on YCB-V it hurts.
\emph{YCB-V does not generalise} in this setting. The trees fit their training frames to about $4^\circ$, but they memorise the cluttered training videos even though test poses lie within $5^\circ$ of training poses. Labels, crops and pose coverage were verified (\cref{sec:supp_ycbv}). YCB-V training normally adds synthetic renders, which our protocol did not use.
\emph{Accuracy is below fine-tuned diffusion.} On our 7 T-LESS objects, LiePose reports R@2/5/10 of 34.9/78.2/88.9\%, from its per-object table, whereas we reach 4.0/36.0/73.5\%. On SYMSOL-T it reports about $0.4$--$0.6^\circ$, against our $2.6^\circ$~\cite{hsiao2024liepose}. Part of this gap is the $2.5^\circ$ leaf quantisation; part is frozen features and per-object data. LiePose also uses visible-object masks and the full T-LESS test set, so even this comparison is only approximately matched.
