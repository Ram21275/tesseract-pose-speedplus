\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Object pose estimation.}
Learning-based 6D pose estimators regress poses directly or predict 2D--3D correspondences followed by PnP~\cite{xiang2018posecnn}. The BOP benchmark~\cite{hodan2020bop} standardises evaluation on datasets such as T-LESS~\cite{hodan2017tless} and YCB-V~\cite{xiang2018posecnn}, where textureless and symmetric objects remain the hardest cases. Synthetic-to-real transfer is studied in its purest form on SPEED+~\cite{park2022speedplus}, where models train on renders of a spacecraft and are tested on hardware-in-the-loop captures. There, keypoint-and-PnP pipelines with fine-tuned backbones lead~\cite{park2024spnv2,park2023spec,ulmer2023eagernet,park2026spnv3}; the most accurate recent result fine-tunes DINOv3 with LoRA and reports that a head on \emph{frozen} DINOv3 features fails with frequent flips~\cite{church2026vfm}. Direct regressors are faster but less accurate~\cite{ancey2026fastpose,xie2026paidvit,dutta2026spacehop}. We keep the backbones frozen and ask how far selection-aware heads can take them.

\paragraph{Discrete and distributional rotation prediction.}
Uniform $SO(3)$ grids can be built from the Hopf fibration~\cite{yershova2010hopf} or by recursive tesseract subdivision~\cite{kurz2017tesseract}; we adapt the latter to cell-centre representatives with unique paths. Grid- and implicit-density methods model multi-modal rotation posteriors, through MLP scores over grids~\cite{murphy2021implicitpdf,hofer2023hyperposepdf} or equivariant spherical features~\cite{klee2023image2sphere}; parametric alternatives include Bingham mixtures~\cite{deng2022bingham}. SpyroPose~\cite{haugaard2023spyropose} evaluates a hierarchical $SE(3)$ pyramid coarse to fine, and LiePose~\cite{hsiao2024liepose} applies score-based diffusion on $SE(3)$ and handles symmetric objects well. Continuous rotation regression has known representation issues~\cite{zhou2019continuity}. Riemannian~\cite{chen2024rfm} and discrete~\cite{gat2024dfm} flow matching provide generative alternatives; we tested both as refiners and report them as negative results.

\paragraph{Consistency, test-time augmentation and distillation.}
Averaging predictions over transformed inputs is a standard route to test-time robustness, and four-rotation test-time augmentation is also used for spacecraft keypoints~\cite{church2026vfm}. Data distillation~\cite{radosavovic2018data} turns multi-transform ensembles into training targets, a form of knowledge distillation~\cite{hinton2015distilling}. Equivariant architectures build rotation consistency into the network~\cite{klee2023image2sphere}. Our orbit voting is a test-time augmentation over in-plane rotations, and orbit distillation is a data-distillation variant. What is specific here is the exact camera-roll transform for crops of off-axis objects, scoring with hierarchical tree likelihoods rather than averaging point estimates, and the target: a diagnosed mode-selection failure.
